SPAN 86400 – Narconarrativas en la frontera México-Estados Unidos
GC: Monday, 6:30 p.m. – 8:30 p.m., Prof. Oswaldo Zavala

El seminario examinará la articulación y el despliegue del discurso securitario estadounidense sobre la “guerra contra el narco” en Latinoamérica entendida como una política de violencia estatal militar trasnacional refractada en una amplia gama de productos culturales y mediáticos (cine, música popular, ficción literaria y periodismo) en comunidades de ambos lados de la frontera entre Estados Unidos y México. Desde una perspectiva multidisciplinaria, estudiaremos representaciones de violencias relacionadas con el narcotráfico y el “narcoterrorismo”, el feminicidio y el juvenicidio, la migración irregular, la pandemia del COVID-19, la industria extractiva, el ecocidio y las tecnologías de vigilancia militarista que convergen en las regiones fronterizas en conflicto. Consideraremos ficción literaria (Yuri Herrera, Juan Villoro, Imanol Caneyada), cine (Alex Rivera, Everardo González, Amat Escalante), música popular (Cartel de Santa, Peso Pluma, Natanael Cano) y el periodismo narrativo (Julián Cardona, Antony Loewenstein, Todd Miller). La discusión se enmarcará en la crítica al discurso neoliberal, las tecnopolíticas de seguridad, el complejo militar-industrial-cultural y los estudios fronterizos.

El curso se imparte en español. En persona.

The seminar will examine the articulation and deployment of the U.S. securitarian discourse on the “war on drugs” in Latin America, understood as a policy of transnational state and military violence refracted through a wide range of cultural and media products (film, popular music, literary fiction, and journalism) in communities on both sides of the U.S.–Mexico border. From a multidisciplinary perspective, we will study representations of violence related to drug trafficking and “narcoterrorism,” femicide and youth homicide, irregular migration, the COVID‑19 pandemic, the extractive industry, ecocide, and militarized surveillance technologies that converge in conflict-ridden border regions. We will consider literary fiction (Yuri Herrera, Fernanda Melchor, Juan Villoro, Imanol Caneyada), film (Alex Rivera, Everardo González, Amat Escalante), popular music (Cartel de Santa, Peso Pluma, Natanael Cano), and narrative journalism (Julián Cardona, Antony Loewenstein, Todd Miller). The discussion will be framed by critiques of neoliberal discourse, security technopolitics, the military-industrial-cultural complex, and border studies.

The course is conducted in Spanish. In person.

SPAN 70100 – Spanish as a Historical Problem
GC: Tuesday, 11:45 a.m. – 1:45 p.m., Prof. José del Valle

Este curso propone un recorrido por articulaciones varias de la lengua, la política y la historia; una lectura a contrapelo de las estrategias de constitución del objeto “lengua” bajo condiciones diversas. La perspectiva teórica que se adopta presume la relación indisociable entre lenguaje y política, e invita a atender a las ideologías de la comunicación verbal y a estudiar reflexivamente las instituciones que la configuran como objeto. La adopción de esta mirada nos obliga a examinar las condiciones materiales de producción y recepción de esos discursos metalingüísticos y a tener presente nuestra propia posición como investigadoras e investigadores universitarios. La propuesta consiste en abordar debates culturales (en torno, por ejemplo, a la figura del letrado y la clase intelectual), maniobras geoestratégicas (como por ejemplo la promoción de un imaginario panhispánico) y formas del discurso político (como el insulto en un contexto de posverdad) a través del prisma ofrecido por la lengua como artefacto político, práctica social y representación ideológica

Este curso impartirá en español. Solo para estudiantes de LAILAC debido a que es un requisito.

This course explores various intersections of language, politics, and history, offering a "counter-reading" of how the "language" object is constructed under different conditions. The theoretical perspective assumes an inseparable bond between language and politics, inviting us to examine the ideologies of verbal communication and to reflexively study the institutions that shape it.

Adopting this lens requires us to examine the material conditions behind the production and reception of metalinguistic discourses while remaining mindful of our own positions as university researchers. The proposal covers cultural debates (such as the figure of the man of letters and the intellectual class), geostrategic maneuvers (like the promotion of a pan-Hispanic imaginary), and forms of political discourse (such as insults in a post-truth context) through the prism of language as a political artifact, social practice, and ideological representation.

The course is conducted in Spanish. Only for LAILAC Students as this is a required course.

SPAN 80200 – Studies in Spanish Instruction
GC: Wednesday, 2:00 p.m. – 4:00 p.m., Prof. Beatriz Lado

Este curso ofrece una introducción a los fundamentos teóricos y metodológicos de la enseñanza y del aprendizaje del español como lengua adicional y lengua de herencia. Se examinan las principales teorías de adquisición de lenguas y enfoques pedagógicos contemporáneos, incluyendo el aprendizaje basado en tareas, el aprendizaje por proyectos y el enfoque de multiliteracidad, así como su aplicación al desarrollo de las competencias lingüísticas, discursivas y pragmáticas. Se exploran estrategias para la enseñanza de las distintas destrezas y la integración de la gramática y el léxico a partir de contextos socioculturales y sociolingüísticos significativos.

El curso también introduce perspectivas recientes en lingüística aplicada y pedagogía crítica, con un enfoque en la justicia social, y considera cómo las ideologías lingüísticas, las jerarquías sociales y las dinámicas de poder influyen en la enseñanza y el aprendizaje de lenguas. Se examinan enfoques que incorporan la conciencia lingüística crítica, la equidad, la inclusión y el uso ético de la tecnología en diversos contextos educativos.

Una parte central del curso está dedicada al desarrollo de materiales didáctivos, incluyendo la creación de programas de estudio, lecciones y herramientas de evaluación adaptadas a distintos contextos de enseñanza del español.

El curso se imparte en español con lecturas y materiales en inglés y español. Lxs estudiantes pueden participar y completar el trabajo del curso en español o inglés.

This course offers an introduction to the theoretical and methodological foundations of the teaching and learning of Spanish as an additional language and as a heritage language. It examines major theories of language acquisition and contemporary pedagogical approaches, including task-based learning, project-based learning, and the multiliteracies approach, and their application to the development of linguistic, discursive, and pragmatic competencies. We explore strategies for practicing language skills and integrating grammar and vocabulary through meaningful sociocultural and sociolinguistic contexts.

The course also introduces recent perspectives in applied linguistics and critical pedagogy, with a focus on social justice, and considers how language ideologies, social hierarchies, and power dynamics influence language teaching and learning. It examines approaches that incorporate critical language awareness, equity, inclusion, and the ethical use of technology across diverse educational contexts.

A central component of the course is devoted to developing instructional materials, including designing curricula, lessons, and assessment tools adapted to different contexts of Spanish-language teaching.

The course is taught in Spanish, with readings and materials in both English and Spanish. Students may participate and complete course assignments in either Spanish or English.

SPAN 87000 – Lorca, Buñuel, Dalí: Theatre, Cinema Painting
GC: Wednesday, 4:15 p.m. – 6:15 p.m., Prof. Paul Julian Smith
(Cross-listed with Comparative Literature)

El curso, impartirá en inglés y no requiere conocimientos de lectura en otros idiomas, aborda el teatro de Federico García Lorca, el cine de Buñuel y algunas obras de arte de Dalí. Asimismo, implica una lectura minuciosa de los textos dramáticos, cinematográficos y artísticos, así como el análisis del voluminoso y contradictorio corpus crítico existente sobre dichos textos. También aborda cuestiones tales como la tradición y la modernidad; la ciudad y el campo; y el género biográfico (*biopic*) en el cine y la televisión. La cuestión de la intermedialidad —o la relación entre diferentes medios— se examinará en sus dimensiones históricas y teóricas. La evaluación del curso se basará en un trabajo final (50 %), un examen parcial (25 %) y una presentación sobre el trabajo final, junto con las publicaciones semanales en el sitio web del curso y la participación oral en clase (25 %). Tanto el examen como el trabajo final podrán redactarse en inglés o en español.

This course, which is taught in English and requires no reading knowledge of other languages, treats the drama of Federico García Lorca, the films of Buñuel, and some fine art works by Dalí. It also involves close reading of the dramatic, cinematic and fine art texts and analysis of the voluminous and contradictory body of criticism on those texts. It also addresses such questions as tradition and modernity; the city and the country; and the biopic in film and television. The question of intermediality, or the relation between different media, will be examined in its historical and theoretical dimensions. The course will graded by final paper (50%), midterm exam (25%), and presentation on the final paper, weekly postings to course website and oral contribution to class (25%). The exam and final paper may be written in English or Spanish.

SPAN 80100 – Autoridad lingüística y producción lexicográfica del español
GC: Thursday, 2:00 p.m. – 4:00 p.m., Prof. Carolina Chaves

Este seminario examina el diccionario como artefacto discursivo y como espacio privilegiado de articulación entre lengua y poder. Desde el marco de la lexicografía crítica, el curso analiza la producción, legitimación e institucionalización de saberes lingüísticos en el mundo hispanohablante, prestando especial atención a las ideologías que subyacen a la selección, definición y marcación del léxico codificado en forma de diccionario.

A lo largo del semestre se estudiarán procesos históricos de diccionarización en España y América Latina, atendiendo a las relaciones de jerarquía, circulación y legitimación que han configurado la tradición lexicográfica hispánica. Se examinará la relación entre lexicografía y construcción nacional, el tratamiento de indigenismos y variedades regionales, así como los debates en torno a los diccionarios de americanismos y las tensiones entre los repertorios producidos en España y aquellos elaborados en América Latina. Asimismo, se abordarán metodologías para el análisis ideológico de la macroestructura, microestructura e infraestructura de los diccionarios, considerando las implicaciones culturales, sociales e institucionales de sus decisiones editoriales.

El curso se imparte en español con lecturas y materiales en inglés y español. Lxs estudiantes pueden participar y completar el trabajo del curso en español o inglés.

This seminar examines the dictionary as a discursive artifact and as a privileged site where language and power intersect. From the perspective of critical lexicography, the course analyzes the production, legitimization, and institutionalization of linguistic knowledge in the Spanish-speaking world, paying particular attention to the ideologies that underlie the selection, definition, and labeling of the lexicon codified in dictionaries.

Throughout the semester, students will study historical processes of dictionary-making in Spain and Latin America, focusing on the relations of hierarchy, circulation, and legitimacy that have shaped the Hispanic lexicographic tradition. The course will examine the relationship between lexicography and nation-building, the treatment of indigenous terms and regional varieties, as well as debates surrounding dictionaries of Americanisms and the tensions between lexicographic repertories produced in Spain and those developed in Latin America. In addition, the seminar will introduce methodologies for the ideological analysis of the macrostructure, microstructure, and infrastructure of dictionaries, considering the cultural, social, and institutional implications of their editorial decisions.

The course is taught in Spanish, with readings and materials in both English and Spanish. Students may participate and complete course assignments in either Spanish or English.

SPAN 87000 – Ficciones maternas. Un abordaje formal
GC: Friday, 2:00 p.m. – 4:00 p.m., María Julia Rossi
(Cross-listed with WGS)

Este curso ofrece herramientas para un riguroso análisis formalista de la proliferante ficción contemporánea sobre la maternidad. A través de un estudio en profundidad de las obras fundamentales de la crítica formalista de principios del siglo XX, examinaremos cómo se constituyen las narrativas maternas en los planos de la forma, el estilo y la técnica literaria. Centrado en la cuestión de cómo se produce la literatura materna como literatura, el curso da prioridad a la lectura atenta y al análisis formal, al tiempo que sitúa esta perspectiva en diálogo crítico con enfoques temáticos y contextuales. Los textos principales procederán principalmente de la ficción en lengua española. Las estudiantes podrán optar por incorporar obras anglófonas o explorar intereses temáticos afines. El programa incorporará además intervenciones teóricas que amplían y reconfiguran los paradigmas formalistas de maneras diversas y significativas. Las estudiantes pueden participar y realizar las tareas del curso en español o en inglés.

Se valora la comprensión del español. Los estudiantes pueden participar y completar el curso en español o en inglés. Modalidad presencial.

This course provides tools for a rigorous formalist analysis of the burgeoning body of contemporary fiction on motherhood. Through an in-depth study of the seminal works of early 20th-century formalist criticism, we will examine how maternal narratives are constructed in terms of form, style, and literary technique. Focusing on the question of how motherhood literature is produced as literature, the course prioritizes close reading and formal analysis, while placing this perspective in critical dialogue with thematic and contextual approaches. The primary texts will be drawn mainly from Spanish-language fiction. Students may choose to incorporate English-language works or explore related thematic interests. The program will also incorporate theoretical interventions that expand and reconfigure formalist paradigms in diverse and significant ways. Receptive knowledge of Spanish is welcome. Students can participate and complete the coursework in Spanish or English.

Receptive knowledge of Spanish is welcome. Students can participate and complete the coursework in Spanish or English

SPAN 87200 – Sociolinguistic imagination and temporalities in contemporary Catalonia
GC: September 14 – 17, 9:30 a.m. – 11:30 a.m. Prof. María Rosa Garrido Sardá
(Rodoreda Chair) In Person

This seminar examines the role of imagination in sociolinguistic practices, ideologies and trajectories in contemporary Catalonia. The selected studies focusing on leisure, education and activism adopt a critical lens on social reproduction and transformation through language. These serve as an entry point to address methodological issues in combining ethnography and historiography to make sense of the nexus between narratives about past, present linguistic practices and imagined futures.

The course material will be mainly in English. Students may participate in class in either/both English and Spanish.

Este seminario examina el papel de la imaginación en las prácticas, ideologías y trayectorias sociolingüísticas en la Cataluña contemporánea. A través de estudios seleccionados sobre el ocio, la educación y el activismo, se adopta una perspectiva crítica sobre la reproducción y transformación social mediante el lenguaje. Estos temas sirven como punto de partida para abordar cuestiones metodológicas sobre la combinación de la etnografía y la historiografía, con el fin de dar sentido al nexo entre las narrativas sobre el pasado, las prácticas lingüísticas presentes y los futuros imaginados.

El material del curso será principalmente en inglés. Los estudiantes pueden participar en clase en inglés, español o en ambos idiomas.

SPAN 87200 – Miradas al multilingüismo desde los márgenes
GC: October 5 – 9, 9:30 a.m. – 11:30 a.m. Prof. Oihana Leonet 
(Atxaga Chair) In Person

El seminario propone una reflexión sociolingüística y educativa sobre el multilingüismo contemporáneo como perspectiva clave para comprender las transformaciones sociales, políticas y académicas actuales, a partir del contexto vasco y en diálogo con realidades latinoamericanas y latinas. El euskera, como lengua históricamente minorizada y hoy presente en ámbitos institucionales relevantes, constituye un espacio propicio para reflexionar sobre el multilingüismo y abordar cuestiones vinculadas con la educación, la identidad, la desigualdad y la justicia lingüística. Esta reflexión parte de la premisa de que los conceptos sociolingüísticos no son categorías estables ni aplicables de forma uniforme (Makoni y Pennycook, 2023).

Uno de los ejes del seminario es la centralidad del sujeto hablante en la sociolingüística contemporánea, desplazando enfoques centrados en el código (Block, 2019). Desde esta perspectiva, los repertorios lingüísticos y las identidades se entienden como resultado de procesos de negociación situados, atravesados por ideologías lingüísticas y relaciones de poder (Martín Rojo y Pujolar, 2020), con atención a categorías como neohablantes o bilingües emergentes. Asimismo, se abordan los paisajes lingüísticos como espacios que reflejan dinámicas de poder y convivencia en contextos educativos multilingües (Gorter y Cenoz, 2023). Finalmente, se analiza la educación como espacio clave en la gestión del multilingüismo.

El curso se imparte en español con lecturas y materiales en inglés y español. Lxs estudiantes pueden participar y completar el trabajo del curso en español o inglés.

The seminar proposes a sociolinguistic and educational reflection on contemporary multilingualism as a key perspective for understanding current social, political, and academic transformations, drawing upon the Basque context and engaging in dialogue with Latin American and Latina/o realities. Basque—as a historically minoritized language that is now present in significant institutional spheres—constitutes a fertile ground for reflecting on multilingualism and addressing issues related to education, identity, inequality, and linguistic justice. This reflection is grounded in the premise that sociolinguistic concepts are neither stable categories nor uniformly applicable (Makoni & Pennycook, 2023).

One of the central themes of the seminar is the centrality of the speaking subject in contemporary sociolinguistics, thereby shifting away from code-centered approaches (Block, 2019). From this perspective, linguistic repertoires and identities are understood as the outcome of situated processes of negotiation—processes that are permeated by linguistic ideologies and power relations (Martín Rojo & Pujolar, 2020)—with particular attention paid to categories such as *neohablantes* (new speakers) or emerging bilinguals. Furthermore, linguistic landscapes are examined as spaces that reflect dynamics of power and coexistence within multilingual educational contexts (Gorter & Cenoz, 2023). Finally, education is analyzed as a key arena for the management of multilingualism.

The course is taught in Spanish, with readings and materials in both English and Spanish. Students may participate and complete course assignments in either Spanish or English.

SPAN 87100 – Naturalezas en tránsito: ecologías del desplazamiento en América Latina
GC: Monday, 4:15 p.m. – 6:15 p.m., Professors: Fernando Degiovanni & Victor Sierra Matute
Course is in Spanish. In person

Este seminario propone una mirada interdisciplinaria sobre las ecologías del desplazamiento en América Latina, desde la época colonial hasta la primera mitad del siglo XX. Pensando a partir de la teoría poscolonial, la historia ambiental y los estudios de movilidad, nos preguntaremos cómo la circulación de personas, ideas y objetos, en contextos de expansión territorial, moldeó los vínculos entre seres humanos, animales y paisajes.

Prestaremos especial atención a la formación y transformación de los saberes zoológicos y botánicos, para entender de qué manera el tránsito y el intercambio —impulsados por intereses económicos— modelaron las representaciones de la naturaleza y su inscripción en discursos de exploración, explotación y resistencia.

Trabajaremos con fuentes diversas: crónicas de conquista, tratados religiosos, políticos y económicos, textos etnográficos y auto etnográficos, literatura costumbrista, herbarios, bestiarios, relatos de viaje, mapas y otros documentos. Estos materiales no solo posibilitan la reconstrucción de rutas y desplazamientos, sino que también sirven para pensar cómo se formaron archivos y contraarchivos en torno a las relaciones ecológicas. Nos interesa observar en particular las tensiones entre texto e imagen en la producción cultural de esos itinerarios. Esta perspectiva nos permitirá reflexionar sobre la ecología del conocimiento y de la movilidad, considerando el papel del género y del desplazamiento en la construcción de saberes, así como la influencia de las epistemologías indígenas en las representaciones coloniales y poscoloniales del medioambiente.

El seminario también incorporará abordajes en torno a la cultura material y la historia sensorial, para analizar cómo los artefactos, los tejidos y las piezas de museo condensan historias de movimiento, adaptación y pérdida. Nos detendremos en la manera en que los saberes indígenas dialogaron con las taxonomías coloniales, resistiéndolas o transformándolas.

El seminario se dictará en español e incluirá lecturas tanto en español como en inglés. Algunos textos del programa están escritos en castellano antiguo o español especializado. Los estudiantes pueden entregar sus trabajos en español o en inglés. Si tienen dudas sobre su nivel de español, deben consultarlo con los profesores.

This seminar proposes an interdisciplinary exploration of the ecologies of displacement in Latin America, from the colonial period to the first half of the 20th century. Drawing on colonial theory, environmental history, and mobility studies, we will examine how the movement of people, ideas, and objects in contexts of territorial expansion has shaped the relationships between humans, animals, and landscapes. We will pay special attention to the production and transformation of zoological and botanical knowledge, analyzing how transit and exchange—driven by economic interests—have influenced representations of the natural world and the inscription of nature in discourses of exploration, exploitation, and resistance.

We will work with a wide variety of materials: from travel and conquest chronicles to religious, political, and economic treatises, including ethnographic and autoethnographic texts, costumbrista literature, herbariums and bestiaries, travel accounts, maps, and other documentary repositories. These records not only allow us to reconstruct displacement itineraries but also to examine the construction of archives and counter-archives around ecological relationships, highlighting the tensions between textuality and visuality in the cultural production of these journeys. This perspective will enable us to reflect on the ecology of knowledge and mobility, exploring the role of gender and displacement in shaping knowledge, as well as the influence of Indigenous epistemologies on colonial and postcolonial representations of the environment.

The seminar will also incorporate an analysis of material culture and sensory history, considering how artifacts, textiles, and museum objects embody histories of movement, adaptation, and loss. We will explore how Indigenous knowledge systems engaged with colonial classifications, sometimes resisting and sometimes transforming European taxonomies.

The seminar will be conducted in Spanish, with readings in both Spanish and English. Some of the texts included in the syllabus require the ability to read earlier and specialized forms of Spanish. SPAN 87100 – (Des)apariciones: historias, formas, escalas
(Cross listed with Women and Gender Studies)
GC: Thursday, 11:45 a.m. – 1:45 p.m., Prof. Ángeles Donoso Macaya
Course is in Spanish. In person.

La noción de “desapariciones forzadas” emergió en el espacio público por primera vez en América Latina en los años setenta durante la Guerra Fría. Esta política de exterminio adquirió carácter regional en noviembre de 1975 con el infame Plan Cóndor. En diciembre de 1978 (a semanas de un significativo hallazgo de osamentas humanas en una mina abandonada en Lonquén, Chile, y con los “vuelos de la muerte” perpetrados por la dictadura Argentina desde 1976 en plena operación), la Asamblea General de las Naciones Unidas formuló un instrumento jurídico para tipificar la “desaparición forzada” (la categoría de “persona desaparecida” estaba estipulada en el Código de Ginebra desde 1977) y dictó una resolución llamando a los Estados a detener esta práctica; dos años más tarde, en 1980, se creó el Grupo de Trabajo sobre las Desapariciones Forzadas. Para entonces, miles de personas habían sido secuestradas, torturadas y desaparecidas por diferentes regímenes dictatoriales en América Latina.

Cincuenta años después, la categoría de la desaparición ha devenido “universal” (Gatti 2011, 2014); efecto de la expansión de las formas y métodos para “hacer desaparecer”, los límites legales de la “desaparición forzada” como instrumento jurídico (refrendado en el año 2006 por la Convención Internacional las Naciones Unidas) son persistentemente “desbordados” (Martínez y Gatti 2020). Estos desbordamientos son el punto de partida de este seminario. Comenzando con la desaparición en el contexto de la Guerra Fría, abordaremos la (des)aparición desde perspectivas culturales y “ecológicas” (Gatti y Casado Neira 2020). Consideraremos la radical plasticidad de esta categoría y su vínculo inextricable con la visualidad, la memoria y el archivo. Exploraremos cómo la literatura, el cine, la fotografía y la performance han respondido a, imaginado y dado cuenta de la desaparición como forma de violencia estructural en Argentina, Chile, Colombia, México, Perú, Ecuador, Estados Unidos y Puerto Rico, desde el desierto de Atacama al desierto de Sonora, en el Océano Atlántico y en el mar Caribe. También, analizaremos sus expansiones y desplazamientos—las formas “no contadas” de desaparecer, las desapariciones de lxs que “no cuentan”.

Algunas de las preguntas que nos orientarán: ¿Qué formas ha asumido la desaparición como fenómeno político, social y cultural en la imaginación literaria, crítica y filosófica? ¿Cómo se visibiliza y visualiza la desaparición de/en el espacio público? ¿Qué formas de borramiento y silenciamiento hacen manifiesta la desaparición en/de los archivos? ¿Qué sucede con la (des)aparición al considerarla en otras escalas, por ejemplo a la luz de las “Inhumanidades” (Yusoff 2020) o del “Negroceno” (Ferdinand 2022), y/o en relación con diferentes espacialidades y ecologías (espacios fronterizos, desiertos, campos de monocultivo, minas, cuerpos de agua)?

Las sesiones y discusiones serán en español; las lecturas serán en español e inglés; lxs estudiantes pueden escribir sus ensayos en español o en inglés.

The notion of “enforced disappearances” first emerged in the public sphere in Latin America during the 1970s, amid the Cold War. This policy of extermination acquired a regional dimension in November 1975, with the implementation of the infamous Operation Condor. In December 1978 (weeks after a paramount discovery of human remains in an abandoned mine in Lonquén, Chile, and with the “death flights” perpetrated by the Argentine dictatorship since 1976 in full swing), the United Nations General Assembly drafted a legal instrument to criminalize “enforced disappearances” (the category of “disappeared person” had been stipulated in the Geneva Code in 1977) and passed a resolution calling on States to halt this practice; two years later, in 1980, the Working Group on Enforced or Involuntary Disappearances was established. By then, thousands of people had been kidnapped, tortured, and disappeared by different right-wing dictatorial regimes throughout Latin America.

Fifty years later, the category of disappearance has become “universal” (Gatti 2011, 2014). As a result of the expansion of forms and methods to “make disappear”, the legal limits of “forced disappearances” as a legal instrument (endorsed in 2006 by the United Nations International Convention) are persistently “overflown” (Martínez and Gatti 2020). These overflows are the starting point of this seminar. Beginning with disappearances in the context of the Cold War, we will broach (dis)appearance from both cultural and ecological perspectives (Gatti and Casado Neira 2020). We will consider the radical plasticity of this category and its inextricable link with visuality, memory, and the archive. We will explore how literature, film, photography, and performance have responded to, imagined, and accounted for disappearance as a form of structural violence in Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Mexico, Peru, Ecuador, the United States, and Puerto Rico, from the Atacama Desert to the Sonoran Desert, in the Atlantic Ocean and the Caribbean Sea. We will also analyze its expansions and displacements—the “unaccounted” ways of disappearing, the disappearances of those who “don’t count.”

Some of the questions that will orient us include: What forms has disappearance taken as a political, social, and cultural phenomenon in the literary, critical, and philosophical imagination? How is disappearance made visible and visualized in the public sphere? What forms of absenting, erasure and silencing make disappearance manifest in the archives? What happens to (dis)appearance when considered in different scales, say, whether in light of the “Inhumanities” (Yusoff 2020) or the “Negrocene” (Ferdinand 2022), and/or in relation to different spatialities and ecologies (borderlands, deserts, monoculture plantations, mines, bodies of water)?

The sessions and discussions will be in Spanish; the readings will be in Spanish and English; students may write their essays in Spanish or English.

SPAN 87000 – Archives of Discontent: The Neoliberal Ethos and Ugly Feelings in Fiction and Film from Central America
GC: Tuesday, 4:15 p.m. – 6:15 p.m., Prof. Magdalena Perkowska

Neoliberal rationality rests on a conditional promise: freedom, happiness, and the good life are attainable—but only for those who fully embrace and successfully enact neoliberal norms and logics across all dimensions of life. Yet, despite these seductive promises of happiness (Ahmed, Cabanas and Illouz, Sztulwark), our historical present—the neoliberal present—is marked by crisis. As Jacques Rancière notes, in the globalized society of the free market, “the crisis is no longer the moment of resolution but the pathological state itself,” a condition that has become normalized, permanent, and generalized (22). This conception resonates with Lauren Berlant’s account of the historical present as a space of multiple overlapping crises that erode the normative vision of a good life. For Berlant, the everyday—work, relationships, routines, health, aspirations, possibilities—has become precarious, shaped by a persistent encounter with diffuse risks, latent threats, and a pervasive sense of disorientation. This precariousness fosters an affective atmosphere of what Berlant calls the impasse.

The impasse, first and foremost, signifies a suspension or entrapment within crisis. But it also names an affective condition—a mode of being shaped by the social, economic, and intimate forces that weigh heavily in the present and obscure the future. As such, the impasse becomes both an experiential horizon and a structure of feeling: a way of perceiving, enduring, and adapting to the ongoing crisis of the historical present, the losses it inflicts, and the sensible disorientation it produces. This condition gives rise to a repertoire of feelings that, with Sianne Ngai, we might describe as ugly, negative, or dysphoric: anxiety, restlessness, paranoia, shame, impotence, exhaustion, disturbance, neutralization, impassivity. These effects do not merely signal personal distress; they are symptomatic of life lived under neoliberal conditions, where the promise of the good life dissolves into a perpetual state of impasse.

Cultural texts from Central America—novels, short stories, and films—produced over the past two decades are saturated with feelings of disenchantment, bitterness, anguish, anxiety, fear, disdain, frustration, sorrow, pain, melancholia, loss, and confusion. These affective markers serve as signifiers of a profound disillusionment with the current neoliberal reaffirmation of market capitalism. This course explores how the impasse—understood both as a suspension in crisis and a negative affectivity—is articulated in a selection of contemporary Central American fictions and films. These cultural texts will be analyzed alongside theoretical frameworks on affect and emotion (Sianne Ngai, Sara Ahmed, Lauren Berlant, Diego Sztulwark, Terry Eagleton, Edgardo Cabanas and Eva Illouz), neoliberalism and neoliberal rationality (David Harvey, Wendy Brown, Franco <Bifo> Berardi, Ricardo Forster, Mark Fisher, Maurizio Lazzarato), and aesthetics (Rancière, Ngai, Ahmed).

SPAN 87100 – The Many Faces of Resentment: Politics of Memory, and the “Great Replacement” Theory in Narratives from the Southern Core and Colombia
GC: Monday, 4:15 p.m. – 6:15 p.m., Prof. Silvia Dapía
Course is in Spanish. In Person.

El resentimiento aparece como un significante flotante en el imaginario político y cultural actual, articulado en contextos diversos como los del populismo; el colonialismo y la decolonialidad; las comisiones de Verdad y Reconciliación, los derechos humanos y las políticas de la memoria; así como asociado también a los movimientos antiinmigración y nacionalistas blancos (teoría del «gran reemplazo»). En la narrativa tradicional del resentimiento, iniciada por Friedrich Nietzsche y desarrollada por Max Scheler, en un mundo dividido entre nobles y poderosos, por un lado, y débiles y sumisos, por el otro, estos últimos se resienten contra los primeros, creando en muchos casos nuevos sistemas morales por inversión de los valores hegemónicos. En lugar de poder y acción, debilidad y pasividad se convierten, según esta narrativa, en virtudes dentro de un nuevo sistema que, al parecer, tiene como núcleo la envidia, el odio y la venganza. Sin embargo, de esta conceptualización tradicional del resentimiento surgen varias preguntas: ¿Podemos reducir el resentimiento a un enfoque puramente psicologista? ¿Qué ocurre si sustituimos este enfoque tradicional del resentimiento por el de su materialidad histórica y lo consideramos un afecto situado y encarnado, más complejo? ¿Qué diferencia hay, si alguna, entre el resentimiento que alimenta «la revuelta de los excluidos» y el resentimiento que canaliza la «revuelta de los olvidados», aquellos que una vez formaron parte del sistema y se sienten empujados a los márgenes? ¿No es acaso posible expandir los usos del resentimiento, redirigiéndolo, dentro de un modelo alternativo, hacia los contextos de conflicto e injusticia a fin de re-imaginar una acción global de resistencia, solidaridad y cambio? 

Dado el resurgimiento del problema del resentimiento en el ámbito político y cultural, propongo discutir estas cuestiones desde una perspectiva latinoamericana. Enmarcados dentro de la teoría crítica, la antropología filosófica, la teoría política, la sociología y los estudios de la memoria, exploraremos diversos enfoques teóricos sobre el resentimiento (como los de Jean Améry, Aleida Assmann, Wendy Brown, Katherine J. Cramer, Gilles Deleuze, Frantz Fanon, Eric Fassin, Eva Illouz, Rahel Jaeggi, Chantal Mouffe, Friedrich Nietzsche, Martha C. Nussbaum, Laura Quintana, León Rozitchner, Max Scheler, Peter Sloterdijk, Natan Sznaider). En este contexto discutiremos ensayos y narrativas de autores del Cono Sur y Colombia de los siglos XX y XXI tales como Juan Cárdenas, Santiago Gamboa, Witold Gombrowicz, Pilar Quintana, Juan Gabriel Vásquez, Pedro Lemebel, Laura Restrepo, Samanta Schweblin, entre otros. Además, también se discutirán películas como Bolivia (2001) de Adrián Caetano, Relatos salvajes (2014) de Damián Szifron y Matar a Jesús (2018) de Laura Mora. El curso se impartirá en español.

Resentment appears as a floating signifier in today’s political and cultural imaginary, articulated in diverse contexts such as those of populism; colonialism and decolonization; Truth and Reconciliation commissions, human rights, and politics of memory; as well as associated with anti-immigrant and white nationalist movements (the “Great Replacement” Theory). In the traditional narrative of resentment initiated by Friedrich Nietzsche and developed by Max Scheler, in a world divided into the noble and powerful versus the weak, the latter resents the former, in many cases creating new moral systems by simply inverting the hegemonic values. Rather than power and action, so this narrative goes, weakness and passivity become virtues in a new system that allegedly has envy, hate, and vengeance at its core. Yet several questions arise out of this traditional conceptualization of resentment: Can we reduce resentment to a purely psychologistic approach? What if we replace this traditional approach to resentment with that of its historical materiality and consider it a situated and embodied, more complex effect? What difference, if any, is there between the resentment that fuels “the revolt of the excluded” and the resentment that channels the “revolt of the left behind,” those who were once part of the system and feel pushed to the margins? Is it not possible to expand the uses of resentment, redirecting it, within an alternative model, to the contexts of conflict and injustice in order to re-imagine a global action of resistance, solidarity and change? 

Given the resurgence of the problem of resentment in the political and cultural arena, I propose to discuss these questions from a Latin American perspective. Framed within critical theory, philosophical anthropology, political theory, sociology, and memory studies, we will explore diverse theoretical approaches to resentment (such as Jean Améry, Aleida Assmann, Wendy Brown, Katherine J. Cramer, Gilles Deleuze, Frantz Fanon, Eric Fassin, Eva Illouz, Rahel Jaeggi, Chantal Mouffe, Friedrich Nietzsche, Martha C. Nussbaum, Laura Quintana, León Rozitchner, Max Scheler, Peter Sloterdijk, Natan Sznaider). Within this framework we will discuss essays and narratives of 20th and 21st century Southern Cone and Colombian authors such as Juan Cárdenas, Santiago Gamboa, Witold Gombrowicz, Pilar Quintana, Juan Gabriel Vásquez, Pedro Lemebel, Laura Restrepo, Samanta Schweblin, among others. In addition, we will also discuss films such as Adrián Caetano’s Bolivia (2001), Damián Szifron’s Relatos salvajes (Wild Tales, 2014) and Laura Mora’s Matar a Jesús (Killing Jesus, 2018). The course will be conducted in Spanish.

SPAN 87400 – Writing in Spanish in the Untied States
GC: Tuesday, 2:00 p.m. – 4:00 p.m., Prof. Esther Allen
Course is in Spanish. In Person.

En gran parte de lo que es el actual territorio estadounidense, el español, por supuesto, llegó antes que el inglés.  El primer inmigrante a Manhattan de quien tenemos conocimiento fue Juan Rodríguez, políglota, traductor y comerciante, nacido en Santo Domingo, de padre portugués y madre africana, que desembarcó de una nave holandesa en 1613 y se quedó a vivir entre los indígenas Lenape. Estados Unidos es hoy el cuarto o quinto país en número de hispanohablantes del mundo, con una población actual de más de cuarenta millones. El español de aquí tiene historiadores como Rosina Lozano y figuras literarias destacadas como José Martí, Julia de Burgos, Giannina Braschi y Lina Meruane. Estas en raras ocasiones entran en los cánones de la literatura estadounidense o en otras simplemente no quieren entrar.  Iniciativas como Recovering the US Hispanic Literary Heritage intentan salvar archivos, periódicos y libros del olvido, mientras que otras más recientes como La Feria Internacional del Libro de Nueva York promueven lo que se escribe ahora y parecen prometer ese gran porvenir caracterizado por Naida Saavedra como el #NewLatinoBoom. Y siempre, en oposicion, yace con su inmenso poder el monolingüismo anglófono dominante.  

La escritura en español de Estados Unidos pone en duda la noción de una “literatura nacional” y tiene el estatus y prestigio de esa “literatura menor” teorizada por Deleuze y Guattari. Este curso ofrece una introducción histórica y teórica a la literatura en español de Estados Unidos, en toda su riqueza.

In much of what is now the United States, Spanish, obviously, arrived well before English. The first known immigrant to Manhattan was Juan Rodríguez, polyglot, translator and trader, born in Santo Domingo to a Portuguese father and an African mother, who arrived on a Dutch boat in 1613 and stayed on to live among the Lenape people. Today, with its more than forty million Spanish-speaking residents, the United States is the fourth or fifth largest Spanish-speaking country in the world.  This country’s Spanish has its historians, like Rosina Lozano, and its notable literary figures, such as José Martí, Julia de Burgos, Giannina Braschi, and Lina Meruane. These latter are rarely included in the canons of US literature, and may in some cases not wish to be included. While initiatives such as Recovering the US Hispanic Literary Heritage seek to preserve archives, periodicals, and books, others, like the Feria Internacional del Libro de Nueva York promote what’s being written now, and seem to promise the great future which Naida Saavedra calls the #NewLatinoBoom.  And always, in opposition, lies the enormous power of the dominant anglophone monolingualism. Writing in Spanish in the United States puts the notion of a “national literature” in doubt, and has the status and prestige of the “minor literature” theorized by Deleuze and Guattari. This course will offer a historical and theoretical introduction to the Spanish-language literature of the United States, in all its richness.

Este curso propone un recorrido por articulaciones varias de la lengua, la política y la historia; una lectura a contrapelo de las estrategias de constitución del objeto “lengua” bajo condiciones disciplinares y políticas diversas. La perspectiva teórica que se adopta presume la relación indisociable entre lenguaje y política, e invita a analizar las formas de concebir la comunicación verbal y estudiar reflexivamente las instituciones que la configuran como objeto. La adopción de esta mirada nos obliga a examinar las condiciones materiales de producción y recepción de esos discursos y a tener presente nuestra propia posición como investigadoras e investigadores universitarios. La propuesta consiste en abordar debates culturales (en torno, por ejemplo, a la figura del letrado y la clase intelectual), maniobras geoestratégicas (como por ejemplo la promoción de un imaginario panhispánico) y formas del discurso político (como el insulto en un contexto de posverdad) a través del prisma ofrecido por la lengua como artefacto político, práctica social y representación ideológica.

This course proposes a review of various articulations of language, politics and history; a reading, against the grain, of the strategies that create the object “language” under different disciplinary and political conditions. The course´s theoretical perspective assumes the inseparable relationship between language and politics and invites us to analyze the ways in which verbal communication is conceived and to reflexively study the institutions that shape it as an object. The adoption of this approach leads us to examine the material conditions of production and reception of these discourses and to bear in mind our own position as university researchers. The ultimate goal is to approach cultural debates (around, for example, the figure of the literate and the intellectual class), geostrategic maneuvers (such as the promotion of a pan-Hispanic imaginary) and forms of political discourse (such as the insult in a post-truth context) through the prism offered by language as a political artifact, social practice and ideological representation.

SPAN 80100 – Imágenes del “Antropoceno”: Visualidad y Medioambiente en América Latina y el Caribe
GC: Thursday, 4:15 p.m. – 6:15 p.m., Prof. Ángeles Donoso Macaya
Course is in Spanish. In Person.

El título de este curso contiene una doble provocación: primero, la del discurso del Antropoceno, cuyo fundamento ha sido extensamente debatido, interrogado y reformulado en las últimas décadas (Wynter 2003; Chakrabarty 2009; Moore 2015; Malm 2014; Haraway 2017; Ferdinand 2021 [2019]; Yusoff 2018, 2024); segundo, la del impulso totalizante del descriptor archivístico “Imágenes de…” también cuestionable (Azoulay 2019). Porque, ¿qué constituyen las “imágenes del Antropoceno”? En la ecología visual de los medios de (des)información, la visualidad del Antropoceno es comúnmente reducida a un muestrario acrítico que goza en la exhibición/contemplación de los devastadores efectos del cambio climático. Imágenes del “Antropoceno”: un catálogo obstinado que (re)produce la retórica de lo inevitable y de lo inefable, que redunda en paisajes apocalípticos (desmoronamientos espectaculares de infraestructura crítica, pilas de embarcaciones varadas en medio de barrios residenciales, comunidades racializadas desplazadas y más). Incluso las “infraestructuras visuales” (Campos Johnson 2018; Campos Johnson y Nemser 2022) habilitadas por el estado como “productor de ambiente” (Parenti 2013) (re)producen y promueven discursos ecologistas y políticas ambientalistas que siguen garantizando y facilitando la expansión del capital financiero, extractivo, racial. Sin embargo, al explorar las prácticas ideadas desde abajo—el trabajo de agrupaciones, comunidades y movimientos articulados en torno a defensa de la tierra y de los territorios en diferentes lugares de Abya Yala—, y también prácticas artísticas y de investigación activista-militante, todas prácticas que, actuando transversalmente o en colaboración, han posibilitado y diseminado maneras creativas de interrogar e impugnar el discurso y la visualidad del Antropoceno, emergen otras formas de ver el tiempo profundo (Farrier 2019) y de visualizar críticamente el Antropoceno colonial. Adoptando metodologías diferentes y mediante diversos modos de diseminación que exploraremos en este seminario (documental expandido, fotolibros, exposiciones itinerantes, charlas performáticas, ficción especulativa, poesía), estas prácticas situadas reorientan los sentidos (no solo la mirada) hacia las capas y capas de experiencias, ecologías y agenciamientos humanos y más-que-humanos, hacia procesos que han sido persistentemente invisibilizados, ignorados u obliterados efecto de la violencia colonial y del capitalismo racial, pero que siguen sedimentándose en diversos espacios—en el fondo de lagunas y del mar, en el interior de minas, en medio del desierto, en bosques y en archivos.

Una selección contundente de textos teóricos y críticos (críticas al discurso del Antropoceno; giro infraestructural en las ciencias sociales y en las humanidades; humanidades ambientales; ecología política), nos ayudarán a iluminar la discusión y el debate. Las clases se impartirán en español; las lecturas serán en inglés y en español; les estudiantes puedes escribir sus ensayos en inglés o en español.

The title of this course contains a double provocation: first, that of the discourse of the Anthropocene, the foundation of which has been extensively debated, questioned, and reformulated in recent decades (Wynter 2003; Chakrabarty 2009; Moore 2015; Malm 2014; Haraway 2017; Ferdinand 2021 [2019]; Yusoff 2018, 2024); second, that of the totalizing impulse of the archival descriptor “Images of…”, which is also questionable (Azoulay 2019). For what constitutes “images of the Anthropocene”? In the visual ecology of mass (dis)information media, the visuality of the Anthropocene is commonly reduced to an uncritical display that takes pleasure in the exhibition/contemplation of the devastating effects of climate change. Images of the “Anthropocene”: an obstinate catalogue that (re)produces the rhetoric of the inevitable and the ineffable, that repeats apocalyptic landscapes (spectacular collapses of critical infrastructure, piles of boats stranded in the middle of residential neighborhoods, displaced racialized communities, and more). Even the “visual infrastructures” (Campos Johnson 2018; Campos Johnson and Nemser 2022) enabled by the “environment making state” (Parenti 2013) (re)produce and promote ecological discourses and environmental policies that keep guaranteeing and assisting the expansion of financial/fossil/racial capital. However, other ways of seeing deep time (Farrier 2019) and of critically visualizing the colonial Anthropocene emerge when exploring practices devised from below—the work of groups, communities, and movements articulated around the defense of land and territories in different places in Abya Yala—as well as artistic and activist-militant research practices, all practices that, acting transversally or in collaboration, have enabled and disseminated creative ways of questioning and challenging the discourse and visuality of the Anthropocene. Adopting different methodologies and through diverse modes of dissemination that we will explore in this seminar (expanded documentary, photobooks, traveling exhibitions, performative talks, speculative fiction, poetry), these situated practices reorient the senses (not just the gaze) toward the layers and layers of human and more-than-human experiences, ecologies, and agencies, toward processes that have been persistently rendered invisible, ignored, or obliterated by colonial violence and racial capitalism, but that continue to sediment in diverse spaces—at the bottom of lagoons and the sea, inside mines, in the middle of the desert, in forests, and in archives.

Several theoretical and critical texts (critiques of the Anthropocene discourse; infrastructural turn in the social sciences and humanities; environmental humanities and political ecology) will help us illuminate the discussion and debate. Classes will be taught in Spanish; readings will be in English and Spanish; students can write their essays in English or Spanish.

SPAN 86400 – Drones, muros, torres y túneles: Culturas de vigilancia en la frontera México-EE.UU 
GC: Monday, 6:30 p.m. - 8:30 p.m., Prof. Oswaldo Zavala

Este seminario examinará las culturas de vigilancia tecnológica y la configuración discursiva de amenazas a la “seguridad nacional” en la frontera México-Estados Unidos. Se estudiará la irrupción de tecnologías como drones, muros virtuales, torres de vigilancia, cámaras y sensores de movimiento y detectores de túneles como parte de la construcción de un estado securocrático que criminaliza poblaciones expuestas mientras asegura la fluidez del capital trasnacional. A partir de lo que el sociólogo Allen Feldman denomina “guerra securocrática” en la era neoliberal, se analizará las implicaciones culturales de tecnologías del complejo militar-industrial global en medios de comunicación y producciones literarias, cinematográficas y televisivas de las últimas dos décadas entre México y Estados Unidos. También se considerará la visualización mediática de túneles, corredores clandestinos y redes de contrabando y trata como parte del mismo andamiaje discursivo del securitarismo fronterizo. El marco conceptual examinará debates sobre la permanente crisis capitalista militar (Mark Fisher, Pierre Dardot y Christian Laval), violencia neoliberal (David Harvey, Slavoj Žižek, Thomas Nail), militarismo y seguridad (Antony Loewestein, Allen Feldman, Todd Miller), muros virtuales y túneles (Wendy Brown, Camilla Fojas, Juan Llamas-Rodríguez), feminicidio (Sayak Valencia, Rita Segato), necropolítica y estado policial (Achille Mbembe, Peter Andreas).

This seminar will examine the cultures of technological surveillance and the discursive configuration of threats to “national security” at the U.S.-Mexico border. The irruption of technologies such as drones, virtual walls, surveillance towers, cameras, motion sensors, and tunnel detectors will be studied as part of the construction of a securocratic state that criminalizes exposed populations while ensuring the fluidity of transnational capital. Based on what sociologist Allen Feldman calls “security war” in the neoliberal era, we will analyze the cultural implications of technologies of the global military-industrial complex in the media, along with fiction, cinema, and television productions of the last two decades between Mexico and the United States. The mediatization of tunnels, clandestine corridors for human smuggling, and trafficking networks, will also be considered as part of the same discursive structure of border securitarianism. Our conceptual framework will examine debates on the permanent crisis of capitalism (Mark Fisher, Pierre Dardot and Christian Laval), neoliberal violence (David Harvey, Slavoj Žižek, Thomas Nail), militarism and security (Antony Loewestein, Allen Feldman, Todd Miller), virtual walls and tunnels (Wendy Brown, Camilla Fojas, Juan Llamas-Rodríguez), femicide (Sayak Valencia, Rita Segato), necropolitics and the police state (Achille Mbembe, Peter Andreas).

SPAN 87100 – Fragments and Silences: Archive, Memory and the Latinx Past 
GC: Wednesday, 4:15 p.m. – 6:15 p.m., Prof. Vanessa Pérez-Rosario

With a focus on both archival theory and method, this course will explore new modes of thinking about the Latinx past by legitimating communal memory, and by engaging models of temporality that do not presume common origins or unbroken continuities and traditions through the study of the multilingual documents and multimedia objects that constitute the Latinx archive. Revisiting Foucault and Derrida’s critical considerations on the archive, José Esteban Muñoz’s provocation that we consider ephemera as evidence, alongside the work of critical archive scholars, we will reflect on the nature of things, fragments, evidence, and acts associated with archiving. This course will tackle the timely research questions: how do we read the migrant and multilingual archives of latinidad that demand a transnational framework? What kinds of collective memory are being made available to address the US’s increasingly Latinx future? How might models of a collective Latinx past engage the memory of both oppressor and oppressed, enslaver and enslaved, those who seize land and those dispossessed of it? To some, the sheer variety of latinidades militates against any commonality. Can we meaningfully connect these variegated experiences of and with the past, without reducing them to a false singularity or continuity? We will read works that comprise the Latinx past by authors such as Luisa Capetillo, Julia de Burgos, María Ruiz Amparo de Burton, and Tomás Rivera.

The course will also explore the theory and practice of speculative history. Contemporary Latinx authors such as Mayra Santos-Febres in Boat People (2005) and Justin Torres in Blackouts (2023) creatively engage and theorize the archive. We will read these contemporary works following recent philosophers of history emanating from the Afro-Caribbean diaspora (Sylvia Wynter, Michel-Rolph Trouillot, Edouard Glissant), and from decolonial Indigenous thought with the aim to address some of the following questions: What ethical imperatives bind those who comingle truth and fiction in historical novels, or experimental poems in which the voice is constrained by an archive that does not come from the immediate family or the lyric self? How is the body an archive; how is space an archive?

Students will be encouraged to investigate unknown or understudied archival documents and fragments by writers of their choosing. The course will be taught in Spanish; Spanish and English can be used in class discussions and final papers.

This course gives students a solid foundation in critical applied linguistics, critical pedagogy, and social justice approaches to teaching L2, L3, Ln, local, heritage, indigenous, and world languages. The course includes a critical overview of language acquisition theories and teaching methods and reflects on where Critical Applied Linguistics and Critical Pedagogy stand within Applied Linguistics and Language Teaching. Students will examine how language teaching and testing often reproduce ideologies, politics, social hierarchies, and power dynamics and will discuss classroom strategies to resist these practices, including developing critical language awareness, antiracist and decolonial pedagogies, gender-inclusive, feminist, and identity approaches, or the use of critical digital pedagogy and AI in the classroom. An essential part of the course will be devoted to creating teaching materials (syllabi, lesson plans, assessment tools) that help teachers and learners understand the socio-cultural, political, and ideological dimensions of language and make them more sensitive to critical and social justice issues.

The course is in Spanish, with readings and materials in English and Spanish. Receptive knowledge of Spanish is welcome. Students can participate and complete the coursework in Spanish or English.

SPAN 87100 – Cuerpos Letrados: Intelectuales, política y performance 
(Cross-listed with Theatre) 
GC: Monday, 2:00 p.m. - 4:00 p.m., Prof. Fernando Degiovanni 
Course is in Spanish

This course will explore how Latin American writers produced intellectual practices beyond the printed page, particularly focusing on performative interventions in public spaces. By analyzing emerging archives, we will look at the way in which modern authors understood their bodies as critical instruments in the construction of alternative political and cultural imaginaries. We will pay special attention to writers’ participation in lectures tours, mass political demonstrations, popular assemblies, and disruptive events. Revisiting Michel Foucault’s critical considerations about the figure of the author, Judith Butler’s reflections on how embodied actions signify prior or apart from any particular verbal demand, as well as theoretical contributions by a variety of performance scholars, we will examine the implications of placing the body of the writer at the center of the visual and audible fields. Debates on the spectacularization of politics, the role of militant cultural agents, and how gender informs the “right to appear” will guide our research. Case studies will include Macedonio Fernández’ conceptual presidential campaign, Manuel Ugarte’s and Salvador Novo’s Latin American interventions in auditoriums and town squares, Norah Lange’s experimental banquet speeches, and Omar Viñole’s street actions with animals. Students will be encouraged to investigate unknown or understudied performance practices by writers of their choosing. The course will be taught in Spanish; Spanish, Portuguese and English can be used in class discussions and final papers. 

SPAN 80000 – Discurso polémico y espacio transatlántico
GC: Tuesday, 11:45 a.m. -1:45 p.m., Prof. José del Valle
Course is in Spanish. Receptive knowledge of Spanish is welcome. Students can participate and complete the coursework in Spanish or English.

Hay un texto en el que Foucault dice que no le gustan las polémicas (Foucault/Rabinow). Emite un juicio moral sobre un a género que, a su modo de ver, interfiere con la búsqueda de la verdad y la interacción con el Otro. Con esta visión, choca la de algunxs analistas del discurso (Amossy) que señalan no solo la compleja estructura comunicacional de este género discursivo sino también su rol en el desarrollo de la opinión pública y su implicación en la vida política. En este seminario, estudiaremos debates culturales y lingüísticos (Blommaert) asociados, en la mayoría de los casos, a las tensiones relativas a la articulación de una esfera pública panhispánica. Nos detendremos, por ejemplo, en la polémica entre Juan Valera y Rufino José Cuervo sobre la calidad de la literatura española y la posible fragmentación del español (c. 1900); en la polémica del meridiano entre escritores españoles y latinoamericanos sobre el estatus de Madrid como referente literario (c.1927); en el intercambio entre Jorge Luis Borges y Américo Castro a partir de las ideas de este sobre el perfil lingüístico del Río de la Plata (1941); y en el agrio enfrentamiento entre Luis Fernando Lara y Concepción Company sobre el valor lexicográfico y simbólico del Diccionario de Mexicanismos (2011). El análisis de estas confrontaciones polémicas se nutrirá de la interacción entre categorías tales como el intelectual (Moraña, Said), la polémica (Amossy), la esfera pública (Habermas), las ideologías lingüísticas (Woolard et al, Kroskrity) y el panhispanismo (Sepúlveda, Del Valle y Gabriel-Stheeman). Y se procederá desde una posición construida con elementos de la glotopolítica, la historia cultural y los estudios transatlánticos.

There is a text in which Foucault says that he does not like polemics (Foucault/Rabinow). He passes moral judgment on a genre that, in his view, interferes with the search for truth and interaction with the Other. This view clashes with that of some discourse analysts (Amossy) who point not only to the complex communicational structure of this discursive genre but also to its role in the development of public opinion and its implication in political life. In this seminar, we will study cultural and linguistic debates (Blommaert) associated, in most cases, with the tensions related to the articulation of a pan-Hispanic public sphere. We will dwell, for example, on the polemic between Juan Valera and Rufino José Cuervo on the quality of Spanish literature and the possible fragmentation of Spanish (c. 1900); on the polémica del meridiano between Spanish and Latin American writers on the status of Madrid as a literary beacon (c.1927 ); in the exchange between Jorge Luis Borges and Américo Castro over the latter's ideas on the linguistic uniqueness of the Río de la Plata (1941); and in the bitter confrontation between Luis Fernando Lara and Concepción Company over the lexicographic and symbolic value of the Diccionario de Mexicanismos (2011). The analysis of these polemical confrontations will be nourished by the interaction between categories such as the intellectual (Moraña, Said), the polemic (Amossy), the public sphere (Habermas), linguistic ideologies (Woolard et al, Kroskrity) and pan-Hispanism (Sepúlveda, Del Valle and Gabriel-Stheeman). And it will proceed from a theoretical perspective built with elements from glottopolitical theory, cultural history and transatlantic studies.

SPAN 87200 – Sexualidades y traducción en América Latina: Importaciones, contrabandos y reescrituras (Cross-listed with Women and Gender Studies) 
GC: Tuesday, 2:00 p.m. – 4:00 p.m., Prof. María Julia Rossi 
Course is in Spanish

Si bien la tradición vernácula de la escritura queer latinoamericana se puede rastrear hasta el siglo XIX, no es hasta pasada la mitad del XX que las formas disidentes del deseo sexual pudieron expresarse abiertamente en la literatura. El principal objeto de estudio de este curso es el rol fundamental que la traducción desempeñó para hacer esta apertura posible. Luego de revisar voces locales anteriores (Julián del Casal, Porfirio Barba Jacob and Salvadora Medina Onrubia, entre otras), nos concentraremos en el subestimado impacto de las traducciones para la expansión de una sensibilidad sexual a mediados del siglo XX a través de un heterogéneo archivo de traducción, tan sugerente como desconocido, que incluye revistas, proyectos editoriales, ensayos y ficciones. Examinaremos instancias en las cuales la traducción demostró ser un recurso vital cuando el deseo queer pugnaba por maneras para decir su nombre, y lo haremos a través de una serie de preguntas: ¿Cómo funcionan las traducciones —textos con una relación ambigua con la autoría, la identidad y el origen— para introducir nuevos discursos, nuevas sensibilidades e incluso nuevas maneras de concebir el deseo en las culturas receptoras? ¿Cómo hicieron las traducciones latinoamericanas para traficar, apropiarse y resignificar los deseos sexuales alternativos y las identidades de género? ¿Hasta qué punto estas importaciones fueron pioneras para crear espacios para que las literaturas locales exploraran otras posibilidades?

While a vernacular tradition of queer Latin American writings can be found as early as the 19thcentury, it wasn’t until the mid 20th century that dissident forms of sexual desire could be expressed openly in literature. The main object of study of this course is the key role translation played in making this possible. After reviewing local voices (Julián del Casal, Porfirio Barba Jacob and Salvadora Medina Onrubia, among others) we will focus on the underestimated impact of translations upon the expansion of a sexual sensibility in mid-20th Century through a suggestive but unknown heterogeneous Latin American translation archive that includes magazines, publishing houses, essays, and fictions. We will examine instances in which translation proved a vital resource at a time when queer desire struggled to find ways to speak its name by looking at a set of questions: How can translations—texts with an ambiguous relationship to authorship, identity, and origin—function to introduce new discourses, new sensibilities, and even new ways of conceiving desire in receiving cultures? How did Latin American translations smuggle, (mis)appropriate and resignify alternative sexual desires and gender identities? To what extent did these imports trailblaze spaces for local literatures to further explore these possibilities?

SPAN 80100 – El diccionario como instrumento ideológico 
GC: Thursday, 2:00 p.m. – 4:00 p.m., Prof. Carolina Chaves O’Flynn 
Course is in Spanish. Receptive knowledge of Spanish is welcome. Students can participate and complete the coursework in Spanish or English

Los diccionarios son discursos ideologizados con presunción de neutralidad. Su materialidad irradia los intereses de grupos dominantes, que estampan en los diccionarios sus propias cosmovisiones y preferencias lingüísticas. A partir de esta convicción teórica, la lexicografía crítica repara en las entradas de los diccionarios y el impacto de sus definiciones en la agencia política de sus usuarios.

El propósito de este seminario es indagar sobre las diversas creencias acopiadas, legitimadas y sacralizadas por, y a través de, los diccionarios, dado el valor simbólico que se les atribuye socialmente. Se examinarán algunas propuestas para el análisis crítico de los diccionarios centradas, por ejemplo, en las repercusiones materiales de las definiciones compendiadas (Lara 1997); la pretensión pedagógica del diccionario (Rodríguez Barcia 2013); su anclaje institucional y legitimador (Aliaga Jiménez 2000); o su confección misma como “acto de intervención glotopolítica” (Lauria 2012). Las lecturas abordarán también tramos de la cronología de los diccionarios latinoamericanos (Lauria 2022; Chávez-Fajardo 2022), así como algunos sucesos de entronización de ciertos usos a expensas de la subordinación de otros. En suma, se revisarán varios de los hábitos ideológicos que motivan (desde la lexicografía crítica) la exigencia de una declaración abierta y transparente, por parte de quienes redactan diccionarios, de los compromisos adquiridos y los intereses políticos representados en ellos (Rojas, 2019).

Dictionaries are ideologized discourses with a presumption of neutrality. Their materiality radiates the interests of dominant groups, which stamp their own worldviews and linguistic preferences on dictionaries. Starting from this theoretical conviction, critical lexicography focuses on the entries in dictionaries and the impact of their definitions on the political agency of their users.

The purpose of this seminar is to investigate the various beliefs collected, legitimized and sacralized by, and through, dictionaries, given the symbolic value attributed to them socially. Some proposals for the critical analysis of dictionaries will be examined, focused, for example, on the material repercussions of the compiled definitions (Lara 1997); the pedagogical aim of the dictionary (Rodríguez Barcia 2013); its institutional and legitimizing anchorage (Aliaga Jiménez 2000); or its very preparation as an “act of glottopolitical intervention” (Lauria 2012). The readings will also address sections of the chronology of Latin American dictionaries (Lauria 2022; Chávez-Fajardo 2022), as well as some events of enthronement of certain uses at the expense of the subordination of others. In short, several of the ideological habits that motivate, from the viewpoint of critical lexicography, the demand for an open and transparent declaration on the part of those who write dictionaries of the commitments acquired and the political interests represented in them will be reviewed (Rojas, 2019).

SPAN 87200 – La condición txarnega: la memoria (im)posible desde un no-lugar

Entre 1950 y 1970, seis millones de personas en el estado español fueron expulsadas de su lugar de residencia por los mecanismos biopolíticos de la dictadura franquista. SPAN 87000 – The Crisis of Neoliberal Present and Ugly Feelings: Aesthetics of Impasse in Contemporary Central American Fiction and Cinema
GC: Tuesday, 2:00 p.m. - 4:00 p.m., Prof. Magdalena Perkowska   

Neoliberal discourse is based on a conditional promise: freedom, happiness, and good life if one follows successfully neoliberal norms and logic in all aspects and levels of one´s life. In spite of these promises of happiness (Ahmed), the neoliberal present (our historic present) is in crisis, a crisis that does not entail optimism.  Jacques Rancière observes that in the globalized society of the free market, “the crisis is no longer the moment of resolution but the pathological state itself”, a ‘normal’ state −permanent and general− of the world (22). A similar conception is embodied in the thought of Lauren Berlant, for whom the historical present is a time in crisis and of multiple crises that threaten the normative concept of a good life. Berlant claims that the everyday –work, social and personal relationships, schedules, scenarios, health, plans and desires, possibilities– has become precarious and transformed into a more or less constant coexistence with an imprecise risk, latent threat, anguish and bewilderment that produce in the subjects a sensation of living (in) the impasse. The impasse means, in the first place, a detention or fixation in a crisis, but it is also an affectivity that corresponds to the social, economic and intimate relationships and conditions produced by the overwhelming weight of the present and the occlusion of the future. Consequently, in the second sense, the impasse is also a form of experience, perception, and adaptation to the crisis of the historical present, the loss(es) it generates, and the sensory breakdown it causes. This means that the impasse produces a sensorium of affects that, together with Sianne Ngai, we could call ugly, negative or dysphoric: anxiety, restlessness, paranoia, shame, impotence, precariousness, neutralization, exhaustion, exhaustion, disturbance, impassivity.

Central American cultural texts (novels, short stories and films) produced during the last two decades are full of such feelings: disenchantment, bitterness, anguish, anxiety, fear, disdain, frustration, sorrow, pain, melancholia, loss, and confusion are signifiers of disappointment with present neoliberal reaffirmation of market capitalism. This course examines the articulation of the impasse –in the double sense of the disappearance of expectations and a negative affective structure− in a selection of Central American fictions and films which will be read in conjunction with theoretical approaches to affect and emotions (Sianne Ngai, Sara Ahmed, Lauren Berlant, Ruth Leys, Franco Berardi, Terry Eagleton), neoliberalism (David Harvey, Wendy Brown, William Davies, Maurizio Lazzarato, among others), and aesthetics (Rancière, Ngai, Ahmed).

SPAN  87100 – The Many Faces of Resentment: Narratives from Argentina and Colombia
GC: Monday, 4:15 p.m. – 6:15 p.m., Prof. Silvia Dapia

Resentment appears as a floating signifier in today’s political and cultural imaginary, articulated in heterogeneous context such as a neoliberalism, post-fascism, right-wing and left-wing populisms, human rights and politics of memory-acquiring thus radically different meanings.  In the traditional narrative of resentment, initiated by Friedrich Nietzsche and developed by Max Scheler, in a world divided into the noble and powerful versus the weak, the latter represents the former, in many cases creating new moral systems by simply inverting the hegemonic values.  Rather than power and action, weakness and passivity, so this narrative goes, become virtues in a new system that allegedly has envy, vengeance, and negation at its core.  Yet several questions arise out of this traditional narrative resentment to “the psychic vicissitudes of weakness” (to phrase it in Wendy Brown’s terms)? What happens if we shift the focus from resentment as a “too psychological” feeling to its historical materiality and regard it as a more complex embodied feeling? What particular nuances does the “weak” and its resentments acquire within the neoliberal narrative and its emphasis on the entrepreneurship? What differentiates the resentment that fuels “the revolt of the excluded” from the resentment that channels the “revolt of the included”, those who were once part of the system and feel pushed to the margins (“dethroned white masculinity”, for example)? Given the resurgence of the problem of resentment in the political and cultural arena, I propose to discuss these questions from a Latin American perspective. 

Framed within critical theory, philosophical anthropology, political theory, and memory studies, we will explore diverse theoretical approaches to resentment (such as Friedrich Nietzsche, Max Scheler, Jean Améry, Albert Camus, Gilles Deleuze, Wendy Brown, Didier Fassin, Martha Nussbaum, Rahel Jaeggi, Katherine J. Cramer, Peter Sloterdijk, and Slavoj Žižek). Within this framework we will discuss essays and narratives of 20th and 21st century Argentine and Colombian authors such as Roberto Arlt, Jorge Luis Borges, Witold Gombrowicz, León Rozitchner, Gabriel Cabezón Cámara, Laura Restrepo, Santiago Gamboa, and Pablo Montoya.  In addition, we will also discuss films such as Lucrecia Martel’s Zama (2017), Ciro Guerra’s La sombra del caminante (The Walker’s Shadow, 2004), and Alejandro’s Landes’s Monos (2019).  The course will be conducted in Spanish.

SPAN 80100 – Language Standardization and Social Inequality 
GC: Tuesday, 11:45 a.m. – 1:45 p.m., Prof. José del Valle

In this seminar, using Ayres-Bellamy and Bennet recent handbook (2021) as a reference for the state-of-the-art in language standardization studies, we will discuss the currency of various theories developed over several decades.  We will approach these theories with a view to understanding standardization's implication in the construction of different forms of social inequality and its potential to support emancipatory efforts.  First, we will focus on the analysis of the classic paradigm as represented by Einar Haugen (1972).  We will examine its connections to the historical emergence of the public sphere (Habermas 1962) and the nation-state (Anderson 1983, Gellner 1983, Hobsbawm 1990); and we will identify the ideal representations of citizen and community explicitly or implicityly invoked in this model.  Secondly, we will focus on critical approaches to language standardization, as a site of power struggles (Joseph 1987), as an ideological process (Milroy and Milroy 1985/2012), as a colonial undertaking (Parakrama 1995), and as an expression of social anxiety (Cameron 1995/2012. We will consider the extent to which these critical approaches offer a better understanding of standardization as a potenially emancipatory strategy.  And we will conclude by assessing the degree to which current research has furthered (or not) the research and views of classic and decades-old-work.

SPAN 87200 – Los Años Radicales del Cine Catalán (1968 - 1978)
GC: Monday, April 11, 2022 through Thursday, April 14, 2022

La muerte del general Franco, en noviembre de 1975, puso fin a cuarenta años de dictadura en España y se erigió en el punto de inflexión de una transición que abarca desde la radicalización de la oposición al franquismo hasta la constitución de un nuevo estado democrático.  Este período que va desde las repercusiones de mayo del 68 a las primeras elecciones plurales en 1978, tuvo su reflejo en el cine catalán.  Filmaciones clandestinas documentaron manifestaciones y asambleas estudiantiles u obreras mientras la censura actuaba férreamente contra películas que denunciaban la dictadura.  Un incipiente marco de libertad permitió en cambio, que cineastas como Pere Portabella, Antoni Ribas, Jaime Camino, Joaquím Jordà o Ventura Pons explorasen el legado de la Guerra Civil, la inmigración social, la identidad nacional o la liberación sexual.  Este curso, que contextualiza el período e incluye la proyección de fragmentos de los films de referencia, analiza el período desde la perspectiva de films indistintamente documentales y de ficción, procedentes de los archivos de la Filmoteca de Catalunya.    

English translation: The Death of General Franco, in November 1975, put an end to forty years of dictatorship in Spain and became the turning point of a transition that ranges from the radicalization of the opposition to Francoism to the Constitution of a new democratic state. SPAN  87400 – Contemporary Latin American Cultural Theory
GC: Wednesday, 4:15 p.m. – 6:15 p.m., Prof. Degiovanni

This seminar will address key theoretical and critical texts that have defined the field of Latin American cultural studies in recent decades. By analyzing the politics of academic knowledge in the global theoretical market, our goal will be to reconstruct both the genealogical lines as well as the epistemological frameworks that have played a crucial role in the region’s current intellectual production. The first part of the course will focus on a corpus of canonical authors that, since the 1980s, contributed to the definition of notions of modernity, coloniality, globalization and the role of the popular in Latin America. In this final section, the course will function both as a seminar and as an academic writing workshop, and will focus on the theoretical interests of the students. SPAN 78200 – Introduction to Literary Translation Studies
GC: Wednesday, 6:30 p.m. – 8:30 p.m., Prof. Allen

In lieu of a welcome video, you’re invited to explore the 2020 online conference “Translating the Future”: https://www.centerforthehumanities.org/programming/translating-the-future

Literature is unimaginable without translation. Yet translation is a disturbing, even paranormal practice, mysteriously conferring xenoglossy upon unwitting or suspicious readers. The literary cultures of English, in particular, have often been resistant to, even contemptuous of translation, or have used it as a tool of colonialism. The problem may lie with prevailing concepts of the original, but translation has often taken the blame. Among the aesthetic, ethical, and political questions it raises — questions increasingly crucial to practitioners of literature worldwide— are: Who translates? Who is translated? What is translated? And—yes—how? And also: what does it mean to think of literature prismatically rather than nationally? What constitutes an anti-colonial translation?

In this seminar, we’ll discuss theoretical and literary readings and engage with the contemporary translation sphere, both in the digital realm and in New York City. We’ll also welcome the perspectives of some notable guest speakers. Students will work towards and workshop a final project, either: 1) a discussion of a specific translation theory or set of theories; 2) an analysis of a specific translation, or comparison of multiple translations, or 3) an original translation into English (of a previously untranslated work) accompanied by a critical introduction and annotation. The class is taught in English, but students should have working knowledge of at least one other language.

SPAN 87000 – Slavery, Gender, and Resistance on Hispaniola
GC: Monday, 4:15 p.m. – 6:15 p.m., Prof. Maríñez

This course examines the institution of slavery on Ayiti/Hispaniola, the island shared by Haiti and the Dominican Republic. It also explores the various modes of resistance that led to its abolition and how various authors have addressed it in their literary works. We begin with theoretical texts on what constitutes slavery, its history, legacy and contemporary forms so as to situate our focus on Hispaniola within a transhistorical, global, human rights context. We then examine the work of historians and critics addressing resistance strategies that took place on both sides of the island and which culminated with the Haitian Revolution, an event of enormous impact on the modern world and on the rise of human rights. Lastly, we analyze neo-slave narratives, that is, recent representations of the experience of the enslaved. Special attention is given to the role of gender and women’s resistance to enslavement through a close reading of novels by Haitian Marie Vieux-Chauvet and Evelyne Trouillot and the poetry of Dominican-American author Ana-Maurine Lara, among others. Readings, papers, and discussions will be in English but students are welcome to read primary texts in either French or Spanish.

SPAN 87200  Cuerpo y alma: Las elegías de Federico García Lorca y Luis Cernuda (Body and Soul: the Elegies of Federico García Lorca and Luis Cernuda)
GC: November 11-13, 2021, Prof. Noël Valis
Time: TBA
(Delibes Chair)

Poema de lamentación, la elegía es un memento mori recordándonos que el dominio de la muerte es infinito, o como escribe Lorca: “¡También se muere el mar!” La elegía puede centrarse no sólo en la muerte de una persona célebre o un ser querido, sino también en otras ausencias, como la pérdida del amor o la destrucción de la convivencia. Dos poesías magistrales iluminan el alcance y profundidad de la tradición elegíaca en la época moderna: el “Llanto por Ignacio Sánchez Mejías” de Federico García Lorca y “A un poeta muerto (F.G.L.)” de Luis Cernuda. Hay otras elegías que también se leerán en este curso, como por ejemplo, de Lorca: “Elegía a Doña Juana la Loca”, “Elegía del silencio” y “En la tumba sin nombre de Herrera y Reissig en el cementerio de Montevideo”; y de Cernuda: “Elegía española I”, “Elegía española II” y “A Larra, con unas violetas (1837-1937)”, donde el poeta observa en plena guerra civil: “Escribir en España no es llorar, es morir”. Estos poemas elegíacos singularizan el poder transformativo de la muerte, que genera, como flores oscuras, otras cuestiones importantes, sobre la memoria, la historia, la violencia, el cuerpo, la sexualidad disidente y el espíritu. Este curso se impartirá en español.

English translation: A poem of lamentation, the elegy is a memento mori, reminding us that the dominion of death is infinite, or as Lorca writes: “Even the sea dies!” The elegy can focus not only on the death of a celebrated person or a loved one, but also on other absences, such as the loss of love or the destruction of social harmony. Two magisterial poems illuminate the reach and depth of the elegiac tradition in the modern period: the “Llanto por Ignacio Sánchez Mejías” by Federico García Lorca and “A un poeta muerto (F.G.L.)” by Luis Cernuda. There are also other elegies we will read in this course, such as Lorca’s “Elegía a Doña Juana la Loca,” “Elegía del silencio,” and “En la tumba sin nombre de Herrera y Reissig en el cementerio de Montevideo” and Cernuda’s “Elegía española I,” “Elegía española II,” and “A Larra, con unas violetas (1837-1937),” where the poet observes in the midst of civil war: “to write in Spain is not to weep, but to die.” These elegiac poems singularize the transformative power of death, as it generates, like dark flowers, other important questions, on memory, history, violence, the body, dissident sexuality, and the spirit. Taught in Spanish.

SPAN 76200 – Theater and Fiestas in Colonial Spanish America: Religion, Politics, and History in Plays and Popular Celebrations
GC: Monday, 6:30-8:30 p.m., Prof. Beatriz Peña

Plays and accounts of public celebrations conform a rich corpus for the scholars of the history of ideologies and mentalities as well as for the history of artistic and literary forms. Colonial celebrations solemnized Corpus Christie; extoled the prestige and the magnificence of the city; memorialized canonizations; honored births, deaths, and weddings in the Spanish royal family; and saluted viceroys and other political figures. These events incorporated plays, rituals, parades, costumes, mythological figures, and indigenous dances, among other elements, that effectively radiated messages and projected the legitimacy and self-justification of the organizers, while also showing their aspirations and pretensions. Moreover, sometimes the authors of plays and the participants in fiesta’s sequences managed to voice resistance and criticisms against the entities in power. The study of surviving plays and accounts of fiestas, set and represented in the cities of Colonial Spanish America, such as Mexico, Lima, Quito, Potosí, and Santo Domingo, in the sixteenth, seventeenth, and eighteenth centuries (Llerena, Ocaña, Estrada Medinilla, Rodríguez Urbán de la Vega, sor Juana Inés, Arzáns de Orsúa y Vela) will allow us to delve deeper into both the imaginary worlds these events put forth and the historical, political, and religious ideas they meant to transmit. The course will also track how earlier celebrations mark the way for the fiesta barroca. The analysis of the encoded political and social imperatives of the primary texts will be supported by works from Maravall, Redondo, De la Flor, Mínguez, Rodríguez Moya, Dean, among others. The class will be taught in Spanish.

SPAN 80000 – Language & Identity
GC: Monday, 2:00 – 4:00 pm., Prof. Cecilia Cutler
 
The course explores the relationship between language and identity by introducing students to the theoretical, methodological, and ideological developments in sociolinguistics for studying how subjects construct, project, and perform different aspects of their identities in interaction. How much agency do people have in choosing and projecting their gender, sexual, racial, ethnic, class, and identities through linguistic, discursive, and other semiotic devices in interaction? How do individuals linguistically and discursively contest the ways in which they are imagined, defined and labeled by others? How can we bring in multimodal semiotic analysis to the study of how individuals construct and project identity? The course will analyze how speakers enact, project, and contest their culturally specific subject positions through communicative interactions and discourses. Topics to be explored include theories and methods for studying language and identity and contemporary topics such as embodiment, racialization and transracialization, stylization, passing, crossing, multilingual identities, second language learner identities, post-coloniality, indigeneity, and race.
 
SPAN 80100 – Transatlantic Polemics and the Panhispanic Cultural Field
GC: Tuesday, 11:45 a.m.-1:45 p.m., Prof. José del Valle 
 
There is one text in which Foucault states that he does not like polemics (Foucault/Rabinow). He passes moral judgment on a genre that, in his view, interferes with the search for truth and the relationship with the other. In a less negative vein, a number of discourse analysts (Angenot, Amossy) have underlined not only the complex communicational structure of this discursive genre but also its role in the development of public opinion and its entanglement in various realms of political life. In this seminar, we will assess the part played by language-ideological debates (Blommaert, Kroskrity, Woolard et at.) in the tensions surrounding the articulation of a disputed panhispanic public sphere. We will focus on the following (among others): disputes over orthographic reform in Chile (c.1843); Juan Valera and Rufino José Cuervo’s clash over the quality of Spanish literature and possible fragmentation of Spanish (c. 1900); the polémica del meridiano between Spanish and Latin American intellectuals over Madrid’s status as a cultural beacon (c.1927); Jorge Luis Borges’ response to Américo Castro’s perception of the River Plate’s linguistic and socio-cultural profile (1941); Martín Luis Guzmán’s proposal, at the first conference of academies of the Spanish language, to reorganize the current institutional arrangement (1951); and Luis Fernando Lara and Concepción Company’s rift over the Diccionario de Mexicanismos (2011). The careful analysis of each polemical engagement will be informed by discussions of the interaction between categories such as the intellectual (Moraña and Sánchez Prado, Said), polemic (Angenot, Foucault/Rabinow, Dascal), public sphere (Habermas, Calhoun), and pan-Hispanism (Sepúlveda, Del Valle). By its very nature, the course will examine the potential of Hispanic transatlantic studies (Moraña, Ortega), that is, the identification of the Atlantic as a dynamic organizing principle in cultural analysis; a topic whose currency in our disciplines was made even greater by the recent publication of Transatlantic Studies: Lantin America, Iberia, and Africa (2019). Room will be made for other polemics that students will be encouraged to identify and discuss. The course will be conducted in Spanish.
 
SPAN 82000 – Maurofilia e islamofobia en la temprana modernidad española, 1492-1614
GC: Thursday, 4:15p.m.-6:15p.m., Prof. William Childers
 
Exploraremos las tensiones culturales desatadas por la Conquista de Granada en 1492 y la imposición del cristianismo en la Península Ibérica a principios del siglo XVI. Empezaremos con el mudejarismo preexistente, manifestado en los romances fronterizos y en prácticas culturales como la zambra y aspectos artesanales: la seda, la yesería, la carpintería, la cerámica y la jardinería. Luego veremos la evolución de la representación de los musulmanes granadinos y sus descendientes a lo largo del siglo XVI, concentrándonos en el período clave entre la Guerra de las Alpujarras (1568-1571) y la expulsión de los moriscos de la Corona de Castilla (1610). Para este período, plantearemos la “cuestión morisca” con cierta complejidad. Leeremos textos de la boga maurófila en varios géneros: novela (El Abencerraje, Guerras civiles de Granada, Ozmín y Daraja); poesía (romances de Lope, Góngora y Lobo Lasso de la Vega); y teatro (El remedio en la desdicha). Yuxtapondremos con ellos documentos de archivo que reflejan el alcance popular del fenómeno, a la vez que los esfuerzos de la Monarquía y la Inquisición por mantener a la minoría vigilada y controlada. Nos asomaremos a ver algunos ejemplos de la resistencia de los propios moriscos: textos aljamiados, los llamados “libros plúmbeos”, y La historia verdadera del rey don Rodrigo de Miguel de Luna. Hablaremos del tema morisco en ambas partes del Quijote y en otros textos cervantinos, irónicamente contrastados con el discurso apologético en defensa de la expulsión (Aznar Cardona, La expulsión justificada, Gaspar de Aguilar, Expulsion de los moros de España y Jaime Bleda, Crónica de los moros de España). Finalmente haremos algunas calas en la historiografía posterior y la actualidad, viendo como la maurofilia resurge constantemente, pero sigue enfrentada hasta el día de hoy con una islamofobia que también es una dimensión permanente, al parecer, de la huella hispanoárabe en la Península. Nos orientaremos partiendo de conceptos teóricos de Bourdieu, Foucault, Pierre Nora, James C. Scott y Stuart Hall, pasados por el filtro de la teoría postcolonial (Said, Bhabha), del colonialismo interno (Aníbal Quijano), y de la “formación racial” (Omi y Winant). Aprovecharemos también los planteamientos de las generaciones de hispanistas que han estudiado estos temas desde distintos enfoques teóricos y empíricos. Este curso se impartirá en español.

SPAN 76200 – Colonial Inquisition: The Dogs of God in the New World
GC: Wednesday, 6:30-8:30 p.m., Prof. Mariana Zinni
 
The Spanish Inquisition is not the well-oiled institution we assume at first time, nor the cruelest and bloodiest one. This course proposes an in-depth study of the ideology and practices of the Inquisition in the New World. We will study the influence of the Holy Office in four mayor fields: its conflictive relationship with the native population, its actions as major censorship agency and regulator of Catholic orthodoxy, its intervention on public and private lives, and its internal affairs. Paying attention to the lives and trades of unknown men and women whose names came to us only through their presence as plaintiffs or victims in legal and inquisitorial records, we will study the sometimes failing repressive apparatus of the Holy Office. By examining inquisitorial and legal documents and secondary literature, we will start to view the collective mentality of the era and its ideological formation. By doing so, we will analyze the influence of the Inquisition on multiple aspects of colonial life -- race, blood purity, sex -- and also the people under its fuero -- Crypto-Jews and heretics, witches and women healers, bigamists, gamblers, curas solicitantes, and false priests, among others.  We will pay attention to the inner logic of the Holy Office in order to illuminate its effects on the political, economic, and social realms of colonial life.
 
We will read original Inquisition records such as AHN 1640, on false wedding celebrated by a false priest, AHN 1030 on bigamy, AHN 1028 on false holy mass and penance, AAL IIA12, on mass simulacrum, documents on spells and love magic, satanic possession and mystic experiences, adultery, sodomy and lovemaking with demons, the trials and punishments of the infamous auto-da-fe celebrated in Lima in 1639, Indian prosecutions –i.e. the polemic one on don Carlos Ometochtzin-, secondary bibliography on Holy Inquisition in both, Mexico and Peru (Greenblat, Alberro, Burns, Castañeda Delgado, Griffiths, Gubovich, Nesvig, Tavárez, among others), as well as critical articles on cases pertinent to the four major topics proposed. 
 
SPAN 87000 – Todo nuevo bajo el sol. Re-Shaping Spanish Identity at the End of the 20th Century
GC: Monday, 4:15-6:15 p.m., Prof. Alvaro Fernandez
 
Following the fall of Francisco Franco's dictatorship, Spain underwent a process of cultural re-imagining, supported both by the political class and the cultural industry. The Francoist image of an authoritarian, conservative society, bearing the marks of 40 years of ultra-catholic fascist dictatorship was actively re-shaped by the political and cultural élite in order to demonstrate that Spain succeeded in conquering its past and turned into a model society of 'modernity'. For the last four decades of the 20th century, the country went through a period of Transition, a process which amounted to the intellectual erasure of the memory of the previous regime. Spain became part of the European Union, which stands for a cosmopolitan, open society, and invested heavily in developing and concentrating its cultural industry. It actively engaged in the economic reconquest of Latin America, and at the end of the century faced the re-emergence of political discussions in the form of historical memory debates.
This course will consist of reading, watching and analyzing cultural products that were created along this transitional socio-political process. It will provide an opportunity to the students to examine and critically evaluate the social, political and cultural aspects of the Transition period and to examine the intersection of culture and politics in reshaping Spanish national identity.
Among other readings, the course will analyze: Aub’s La gallina ciega, Patino’s Nueva cartas a Berta, Espinosa’s La fea burguesía, Rosa’s El vano ayer and Marías’ Corazón tan blanco.

SPAN 87100 – The Neoliberal Promise of Happiness and Ugly Feelings: Post-Utopic Fiction and Film from Central America
GC: Tuesday, 6:30-8:30 p.m., Prof. Magdalena Perkowska
 
In The Promise of Happiness (2010), Sara Ahmed critically addresses the promise of happiness that circulates in globalized society, defining people’s attitudes and expectations. She argues that, as on object of individual and social desire, happiness may mean agreement, going along or even willfully submitting to social norms. In this way, happiness can be used as a shield against the recognition of and engagement with political and social alternatives. In contrast, unhappiness and negativity are affective points of disagreement and, as such, judgmental and non-conforming.
Ugly feelings, as defined by Sianne Ngai in her eponymous study (2005), are “minor and generally unprestigious” emotions of a strong, diagnostic nature, because they have capacity of shedding light on “a real social experience and a certain kind of historical truth.” Central American cultural texts (novels, short stories and films) produced during the last two decades are full of such feelings: disenchantment, bitterness, anguish, anxiety, fear, disdain, frustration, sorrow, pain, melancholia, loss, and confusion are signifiers of disappointment with past utopias and present neoliberal restoration or reaffirmation of market capitalism. This course explores a selection of Central American fictions and films which will be read in conjunction with theoretical approaches to affect and emotions (Phillip Fischer, Sianne Ngai, Sara Ahmed, Ann Cvetkovich, Lauren Berlant, Ruth Leys, Martha Nussbaum, among others), neoliberalism (David Harvey, Wendy Brown), and politics and aesthetics (Rancière).  We will examine unresolved tensions articulated through affects and emotions, and will fathom what commitments, if any, are encoded in these ‘feeling texts.’

This course examines how scientific literature on climate change is discursively framed, how it becomes reframed as it travels to the social spaces where public opinion is negotiated, and how those linguistic and textual strategies shape and are shaped by the political economy of climate debates, that is, by the specific geopolitical and social positions of the different stake-holders. The climate literature produced by the specialized sciences is vast and not easy to transfer, on one hand, to the academic realm of the humanities and, on the other, to the complex public sphere where issues of political importance are selected and debated. We provide a discussion-style class of key emerging issues related to climate change using a critical discourse approach. Capitalizing on two weekly readings, students will infer the discursive features of the climate change debate, aiming at understanding the concepts that revolve around climate change in different political and social contexts. To that end, students will compare the diverse discursive patterns regarding climate change published in scientific journals (e.g. Science) with those published in journals and newspapers oriented towards a wider audience (e.g., The New Yorker). These pairwise comparisons will be drawn synchronically and diachronically. We will also (1) analyze the discursive frameworks in the scientific and public spheres; (2) explore the different mechanisms used to transfer scientific knowledge and social knowledge; and (3) tackle the geopolitical meridional differences in climate change discourses by making students present and comment on specific manuscripts published both in the United States and Latin America.

SPAN 80000 – Language, Identity and Political Economy
GC: Tuesday, 11:45-1:45 p.m., Prof. José del Valle
 
In this seminar, we will examine language´s involvement in the contemporary construction and mobilization of ethnic and national identities as well as in the development of late capitalist forms of economic organization. The sociolinguistic objects and specific case-studies examined throughout the seminar will include, but not be limited to, language revitalization processes in Latin America, the politics of language and ethnic and national identity in the United States, the promotion of Spanish in global linguistic markets, and normalization policies and discourses on behalf of minority languages in Europe -mainly in Spain-.
 
The seminar´s narrative and theoretical footing -anchored in critical sociolinguistics and glotopolítica- will be established through Monica Heller and Alexandre Duchêne’s proposal to analyze the deployment of linguistic ideologies around the 'pride' and 'profit' tropes (Language in Late capitalism, 2012). The studies and the interpretive frameworks put forth in this book will be placed in dialectic relation with each other and with other sociological and political views of language´s interface with capital and labour, identity and citizenship, and politics and power. Various approaches to language and identity will be introduced through John E. Joseph´s Language and Identity (2004); critical approaches to language and political economy will be discussed through Marnie Holborow´s Language and Neoliberalism (2015) and Monica Heller and Bonnie McElnihhy´s Language, Capitalism, Colonialism (2017); and the articulation of language and politics will be studied through John Joseph´s Language and Politics (2009) and Bentivegna, del Valle, Niro and Villa´s Anuario de Glotopolítica 1 (2017).
 
As the seminar proceeds, discussion of each topic will be informed by the following readings among others: José del Valle, La lengua, ¿patria común? (2007); Norma Mendoza-Denton, Homegirls: Language and Cultural Practices among Latina Youth Gangs (2008); Jan Blommaert, The Sociolinguistics of Globalization (2010); Robert Phillipson, Linguistic Imperialism Continued (2010); H. Sami Alim and Geneva Smitherman, Articulate While Black: Barack Obama, Language, and Race in the U.S. (2012); Angela Reyes, Language, Identity, and Stereotype among Southeast Asian American Youth (2012); Jacqueline Urla, Reclaiming Basque: Language, Nation, and Cultural Activism (2012); Elvira Arnoux and Susana Nothstein´s Temas de glotopolítica: Integración regional sudamericana y panhispanismo (2014); Serafín Coronel-Molina, Language Ideology, Policy and Planning in Peru (2015); Kathryn A. Woolard, Singular and Plural: Ideologies of Linguistic Authority in 21st Century Catalonia (2016); Jonathan Rosa, Looking like a Language, Sounding like a Race: Raciolinguistic Ideologies and the Learning of Latinidad (2018). [The seminar will be conducted in various forms of Spanish and English; so, receptive knowledge of both languages is required; class participation and papers may be in any language or languages I think I can understand.]
 
SPAN 80200 – Teaching Spanish as a Heritage Language
GC: Thursday, 2:00-4:00 p.m., Prof. Beatriz Lado
 
This course is an introduction to the field of heritage language education, with an emphasis on the teaching of Spanish to bilingual Spanish-English learners in the US. We will explore different areas that are relevant for the field, such as the definition of a heritage speaker/language; heritage language acquisition, development, and maintenance; historical, socio-cultural, political, and ideological dimensions of heritage language learning and teaching; and current pedagogical approaches to teaching Spanish as a heritage language (e.g., critical, multiliteracies). An important part of the course will be devoted to curriculum development and assessment. The course will be conducted in Spanish with readings in both English and Spanish.

SPAN 87000 – In-Between Worlds & Traditions: Rereading the “Crónicas de Indias”
GC: Wednesday, 6:30-8:30 p.m., Prof. Raquel Chang-Rodríguez
 
This course will study a diverse group of testimonies from the early contact period and beyond.  Generally grouped under the label “crónicas de Indias,” they will include letters, histories, relaciones, and chronicles written by men and women of diverse backgrounds and ethnicity. These works will be situated in their historical and literary contexts in order to analyze the objectives of their authors and understand their meaning in the shared culture and history of Europe and the Americas. Among the issues to be discussed are: 1) how these texts became “literature;” 2) alphabetic culture vis-à-vis native traditions; 3) the polemics about the indigenous population; 4) the eye-witness and the construction of history; 5) the indigenous perception of the conquest; 6) gender issues; 7) Sor Juana’s view of the conquest.  Class discussions will be illustrated with images and communication facilitated through Blackboard. There will be ample time for discussion and pursuing individual projects
Readings will include: selections from letters by Colón, Isabel de Guevara, 2da carta de relación, Cortés* [Castalia or Porrúa]; Bartolomé de las Casas, Brevísima relación de la destrucción de las Indias * (Cátedra), Inca Garcilaso de la Vega, Comentarios reales (Biblioteca Ayacucho, on-line), Felipe Guaman Poma de Ayala, Primer nueva corónica y buen gobierno (selection; Royal Library Copenhagen, on-line), La monja alférez, Catalina de Erauso (Cátedra)*. Other material will be electronically distributed. *Purchase text.
Among the general requirements are: team work, exam, research essay (MLA Style, latest edition; written in English or Spanish), active class participation in English or Spanish reflecting reading of assigned material.
The specific bibliography will be distributed in class.

SPAN 87100 – Del espacio de aca: Photographic Discourses & Practices in/about Latin America
GC: Thursday, 2:00-4:00 p.m., Prof. Angeles Donoso Macaya
 
Traditional histories of photography return again and again to France, England and the United States to investigate the origins of the photographic apparatus, explain the birth of the documentary genre, elucidate the links between control, surveillance and the advent of photographic archives, or explore issues related to the circulation of photographs. Although images produced in Latin America & the Caribbean played a central role in these processes (Poole 1998), regional photographic practices have been persistently ignored (or rendered invisible) in traditional histories of photography. This postcolonial erasure is even more startling if one considers that the birth of photography was the product of a relentless sequence of displacements and disseminations (Azoulay 2008, Cadava 2013). If photography is both an entangling and de-territorializing civil practice (due to its capacity for circulation and movement), it is necessary to critically examine the histories of photography and elucidate the (ignored, silenced, invisible) place of Latin American photographic practices within these same discourses.
The title of this course refers to Ronald Kay’s essay Del espacio de acá: señales para una mirada americana (1980). Kay’s ideas and recent contributions by visual studies and postcolonial and decolonial criticism will serve us to elucidate the notion of colonial visuality (in opposition to that of modern visuality) and to examine the voids and silences of the histories of photography vis-à-vis the photographic practices from the 19th century. We will also review theories about the documentary and the document, the archive, and forensic aesthetics in order to explore and reconsider the prevalent linkage made by photography criticism between 20th-century Latin American documentary photography and humanist ideologies.

SPAN 87100 – Cuerpos letrados: Intelectuales, política y performance
GC: Tuesday, 4:15-6:15 p.m., Prof. Fernando Degiovanni
 
Este curso se propone explorar la noción de intelectual más allá de su producción escrita. Trabajando aspectos usualmente marginalizados en el abordaje de su figura, como sus intervenciones en espacios públicos y masivos, nos planteamos la posibilidad de pensar la actividad del escritor como una práctica corporizada, dependiente de la voz y el gesto y formulada para un público que ve y oye. Esta historia del cuerpo letrado se puede rastrear en salones y cafés, tours de conferencias, discursos en asambleas masivas, entre otros espacios, y plantea interrogantes distintos a los que presupone su análisis como productor de textos destinados a ser leídos. Nociones tales como espectacularización, populismo y género serán claves en este curso. Entre los eventos que trabajaremos se encuentran la campaña presidencial de Macedonio Fernández, el tour latinoamericano de Manuel Ugarte, los banquetes de Norah Lange, y las performances de Ramón Gómez de la Serna y Omar Viñole. El seminario supone el abordaje de estos cuerpos letrados desde la teoría contemporánea como desde la investigación misma de los archivos en los cuales se documentaron sus prácticas. El curso dedicará especial atención a las intervenciones de Omar Viñole, figura sobre la cual convergen algunos nombres citados más arriba. Entendido como un seminario dentro del seminario, el estudio de la producción de Viñole (quien a mediados de la década de 1930 realizó numerosas intervenciones escandalosas en Buenos Aires y Montevideo acompañado por una vaca) permitirá explorar los desafíos específicos que plantea el análisis de la performance en circunstancias históricas y culturales atravesadas por la política de masas, la institucionalización letrada y la emergencia de nuevos debates sobre el cuerpo y la sexualidad.

El curso pretende ofrecer una aproximación histórica al llamado hecho diferencial catalán partiendo de las ideas que las distintas fuerzas políticas propagaron a lo largo de los siglos xix y xx sobre las lenguas de España y el papel del catalán en la escuela. A partir de ejemplos concretos que marcaron los altibajos en la recuperación del catalán como lengua culta y escrita, se observará la inseparable relación entre las ideologías adoptadas y ciertos modelos escolares. Cabe destacar que las conclusiones que se sacarán del estudio de los documentos aportados (discursos, apologías, prefacios, etc.) serán extrapolables a situaciones lingüísticas que presentan cierto paralelismo con el caso catalán. Se fomentará el acercamiento crítico a las diversas realidades e ideas lingüísticas que existen en el mundo hispánico.
 
SPAN 87200 – ‘La batalla del relato’ y el conflicto de identidades nacionales en España
GC: Tuesday, 10/9/18 through Friday, 10/12/2018, 10:30 a.m.-1:00 p.m., Prof. Gorka Mercero Altzugarai, []
(Atxaga Chair)
 
En el debate político actual en España, la expresión la batalla del relato se refiere a la necesidad de que el grupo armado ETA – disuelto definitivamente en mayo de 2018 tras 60 años de existencia – se recuerde exclusivamente como una muestra más de la deplorable capacidad humana para el daño y la destrucción. Pero esta visión deja peligrosamente de lado el hecho de que para muchos ETA fue la consecuencia directa de un conflicto político. Diversas obras literarias han ofrecido su visión sobre este asunto, algunas tan conocidas como El hijo del acordeonista (2003) de Bernardo Atxaga o Patria (2016) de Fernando Aramburu. El curso pondrá dichas obras en su contexto, analizará su posición respecto a la batalla del relato y evaluará su contribución a la prolongación o resolución de un conflicto negado por unos, señalado por otros, y que entre todos debemos intentar resolver.

SPAN 80000 – Todo nuevo bajo el sol: Re-shaping Spanish Identity at the End of 20th Century
GC: Thursday, 6:30-8:30 p.m., Prof. Fernandez, [38050]

Following the fall of Francisco Franco's dictatorship, Spain underwent a process of cultural re-imagining, supported both by the political class and the cultural industry. The Francoist image of an authoritarian, conservative society, bearing the marks of 40 years of ultra-catholic fascist dictatorship was actively re-shaped by the political and cultural élite in order to demonstrate that Spain succeeded in conquering its past and turned into a model society of 'modernity'. For the last four decades of the 20th century, the country went through a period of Transition, a process which amounted to the intellectual erasure of the memory of the previous regime. Spain became part of the European Union, which stands for a cosmopolitan, open society, and invested heavily in developing and concentrating its cultural industry. It actively engaged in the economic reconquest of Latin America, and at the end of the century faced the re-emergence of political discussions in the form of historical memory debates. This course will consist of reading, watching and analyzing cultural products that were created along this transitional socio-political process. It will provide an opportunity to the students to examine and critically evaluate the social, political and cultural aspects of the Transition period and to examine the intersection of culture and politics in reshaping Spanish national identity.
Among other readings, the course will analyze: Aub’s La gallina ciega, Patino’s Nueva cartas a Berta, Espinosa’ La fea burguesía, Rosa’s El vano ayer, and a selection of Latin American literature affected by the Spanish publishing companies’ dominance​.

SPAN 80100 – Analyzing Discourse Data
GC: Monday, 2:00-4:00 p.m., Prof. Cutler, [38048]
 
Discourse Analysis: Studying discourse is more than examining language use; it entails studying the use of language as a form of social practice, and a way of reflecting and shaping society. This course explores socially informed and critical approaches to analyzing language at the level of discourse (beyond the level of the sentence), including an overview of current theories and methods (e.g. Pragmatics, Interactional Sociolinguistics, Conversation analysis, Critical Discourse Analysis, and Computer-Mediated Discourse Analysis). With this set of tools, students will gain experience analyzing different forms of spoken and written texts such as conversations, service encounters, computer-mediated interaction, print/online news and other forms of public discourse. Students will develop their own projects and present their research at the end of the semester focusing on (but not limited to) examinations of micro structural patterns across texts, coherence, turn-taking, lexical choices, and translanguaging, or macro level phenomena such as speaker intentions, and socio-cultural meanings in relation to ideology, identity, power, and gender.
 
SPAN 82200 – The Invention of Love in Early Modern Spanish Poetry
GC: Thursday, 4:15-6:15 p.m., Prof. Schwartz, [38053]
 
The development of Humanism led to the rediscovery of Italian poetry in the Renaissance, which became a main model for different conceptions of love at the time, and of Greek and Roman poetry. The example of Petrarch’s Canzoniere and his Italian followers in the sixteenth-century, Bembo among others, combined with the enthusiastic reception of Neo-Platonism after the translations of Plato and the philosophical writings of Marsilio Ficino, promoted a vision of love that was going to be recreated by Spanish Renaissance and Baroque poets for two long centuries. The purpose of this course will be to examine the relations between literary and philosophical theories and their recontextualization in poetic texts, focusing on the constitution of the voices of the lover and on the portraits of the beloved, as they appear in individual poems, and in the collections built as “cancioneros” after the example of Petrarch. Garcilaso de la Vega’s and Fernando de Herrera’s works, historical precedents of Góngora’s and Quevedo’s poetry, will be studied in conjunction with readings of Neo-Platonic theory, Marsilio Ficino’s treatises and those composed by his most important mediator in Spain, León Hebreo in the famous translation of Inca Garcilaso de la Vega, with references to other important texts in the transmission of Neo-Platonic ideas.

SPAN 87000 – Ugly Feelings: Post-Utopic Fiction and Film from Central America
GC: Tuesday, 2:00-4:00 p.m., Prof. Perkowska, [38055]
 
Ugly feelings, as defined by Sianne Ngy in her eponymous study, are “minor and generally unprestigious” emotions of a strong, diagnostic nature because they have the capacity to shed light on “a real social experience and a certain kind of historical truth.” Central American cultural texts (novels, short stories and films) produced during the last two decades are full of such feelings: disenchantment, bitterness, anguish, anxiety, fear, disdain, frustration, sorrow, pain, melancholia, loss, and confusion are signifiers of disappointment with past utopias and present neoliberal restoration or reaffirmation of market capitalism. This course explores a selection of Central American fictions and films which will be read in conjunction with theoretical approaches to affect and emotions (Phillip Fischer, Sianne Ngy, Sara Ahmed, Ruth Leys, Martha Nussbaum, among others), neoliberalism (David Harvey, Wendy Brown), and politics and aesthetics (Jacques Rancière).  We will examine unresolved tensions articulated through affects and emotions, and will fathom what commitments, if any, are encoded in these ‘feeling texts.’  
 
SPAN 87100 – To Love & To Sin:  Sexual Practices in Colonial Latin American
GC: Tuesday, 4:15-6:15 p.m., Prof. Zinni, [38051]
 
In this class we will examine different sexualities, love relationships and sexual practices in Colonial Latin America. We will focus our attention in three major topics: cohabitation, marriage and homosexuality regarding the Spanish Law and mores. All these three fields point out distinct problems and outcomes that we will explore through the reading of Spanish chronicles, Confesionarios de Indios, satirical poems by Juan del Valle y Caviedes, sections of Juan Rodríguez Freyle’s El Carnero, the autobiography of the Monja-Alferez, inquisitorial testimonies, and archival materials.
The course will be taught in Spanish.

SPAN 87300 – Language and Politics

This course addresses the study of political language in linguistic anthropology. The course surveys classic anthropological works on political oratory, political discourse, rhetoric, as well as ethnographic cases that explore how linguistic phenomena are intrinsic to political systems. This is a writing intensive course in which the students will be required to develop a final project of their interest.

SPAN – Elegies Without Consolation: Territory, Language and Conflict in Contemporary Galician Culture
GC: Monday, 3/5/2018 through Friday, 3/9/2018, Time: TBA, Prof. Helena Miguélez-Carballeira, [38747]
 
Galician culture and politics are marked today by discourses of loss and collapse, which are often related to the idea that Galician identity is on an irreversible course towards disappearance. The historical split of the Galician nationalist party in 2012 and the appearance of new political parties have given way to new discourses about national construction in the region. Galician language planning continues to reap meagre results in terms of language revitalization, with a 2014 study showing that first-language Galician speakers have now dropped below 50% for the first time in history. Current criticism of heritage policies is often linked to the idea that the Galician natural and built landscape has suffered a decades of social and institutional neglect. How is Galician culture registering these trends? And what consolation does it bring about, if any? This course will try to answer these questions and others through an engagement with contemporary Galician poetry, fiction, essay and film.   
 
SPAN – Palabra e historia. La poesía de Jaime Gil de Biedma
GC: Dates: TBA, Time: TBA, Prof. Luis García Montero, [38748]
 
Jaime Gil de Biedma (1929-1990) es uno de los poetas más importantes de la literatura española en la segunda mitad del siglo XX. Su figura se proyectó en la generación de los años 80 con la misma fuerza que Juan Ramón Jiménez dejó su magisterio en la generación del 27. Este tipo de herencias tienen su razón de ser no sólo en la calidad estética, sino en la configuración de una personalidad capaz de reunir buena parte de los debates más serios en el horizonte lírico de su tiempo. Heredero de Jorge Guillén, el mundo poético de Gil de Biedma se concibió a sí mismo como una superación del simbolismo y como acercamiento al realismo crítico y a la poesía meditativa. Para este tránsito buscó un diálogo fecundo con la tradición anglosajona (Eliot y Auden, sobre todo).
 
Los poemas de Jaime Gil de Biedma son una indagación en la historia literaria, en la historia social de España y en su propia identidad homosexual. Su estudio se abre por ello a cuestiones como la intertextualidad, la ironía, el compromiso político y el carácter histórico de la intimidad. El silencio prematuro de Gil de Biedma, analizado por él mismo, supone uno de los síntomas literarios más lúcidos de lo que significó para la escritura el proceso culturas y social de la Transición española.
 
SPAN 87200-  Nuevas visiones del parentesco y de la comunidad desde la literatura y el cine
GC: Dates: April 9-13, 2018, Time: 11:00 - 1:00 pm. Prof. Marta Segarra [38919]
(Cátedra Rodoreda)

Durante el último medio siglo, los conceptos tanto de “comunidad” como de “parentesco” han sufrido profundos cambios en las sociedades occidentales. Desde los estudios de género y sexualidad se abogó por una extensión de la “familia” a grupos de personas unidas por lazos distintos a los de sangre. Paralelamente, diversas líneas de pensamiento han problematizado la noción de “comunidad” y de “lo común”. Analizaremos en este seminario diversas formas que toman estas nuevas visiones del parentesco y de la comunidad en textos literarios y cinematográficos, pertenecientes a la cultura catalana y europea.
